## 1. The dramatic promise — the thing viewers come back for
**Vietnamese promise:** "Mỗi sáng là một gia đình bình thường. Mỗi tiếng chuông có thể đưa họ tới một thế giới không ai ngờ. Họ giải cứu một câu chuyện nhỏ ở đó và tiến gần hơn đến việc hiểu ai đã khiến hàng nghìn gia đình Veylora phải xa nhau."

**English A2 promise:** "Our home is here. But someone is lost out there. Every bell has a story."

**Core logline:** When the inter-world bell network of Veylora is deliberately sabotaged, Mara's mixed ordinary/magical family protects its everyday life in Bellfern while helping stranded travelers and solving problems in unpredictable worlds. Each verified trace helps Orin investigate the broken network and search for missing relatives; a more dangerous question gradually appears: did someone destroy the network simply to hide people, or to gain control of where they could ever go?

**Genre engine:** family fantasy + mystery adventure + warm humor + emotionally honest character drama + contextual slow-English A2. Fantasy is the entertainment, not decoration for a vocabulary lesson. A2 is the spoken-language accessibility constraint, not the intellectual complexity of the plot.

**Central dramatic question:** Can the Bell family reunite people and reconnect worlds *without rebuilding the same system that made them vulnerable*?

**Theme:** "Home is not only a place you return to. Home is a place where you are welcomed — and where you may choose to stay." Countertheme: "To keep everyone safe, someone must control every door." Neither side is settled by an easy speech; choices carry costs.

## 2. What has happened before episode one
Veylora was once the hub of a distributed bell route system connecting many inhabited worlds. A bell is not a teleportation wish: destination, anchor, permission and a safe return matter. Some communities relied on routes for food, letters, care and reunions. An intentional disruption during **the Night of the Silent Bells** isolated many connections. Travelers were separated and registries were damaged or altered. We know sabotage occurred; **the culprit, motive, sequence and casualties are NOT preapproved facts**.

Orin, a leading wizard and Mara's father, travels with his own working bell, following leads and helping displaced people. He could return home physically, but sometimes chooses to follow time-sensitive leads; that choice is an emotional cost, not an impossibility excuse. Mara holds the family line in Bellfern and manages the concealed workshop/home bell. Lia and Juno attend normal Earth school, Evan works at the post office, and Evan's parents live nearby. Everyone returns to Bellfern as the recurring emotional home base, except Orin when his investigation requires otherwise.

Episode 1 remains the established **Aila rescue and choice-of-home pilot**: Orin finds Aila, hands her a preconfigured limited personal bell, and pursues a departing ferry clue; Aila arrives at the Bell family with an unruly flying suitcase, and chooses a safe home rather than being assigned to one. EP006, EP007 and EP016 are developments AFTER this, not repeated first rescues or first bell grants. There must be a real family dinner and a self-contained earned payoff.

## 3. Three nested story engines
**A. Home engine (every episode):** What will the family lose, learn or choose *today*? Meals, school, friends, grandparents, Evan's ordinary work, Mara's workshop and family promises anchor feelings for both children and adult viewers. Never make every Earth scene a secret-keeping joke.

**B. World engine (most episodes):** A new world or a truly supernatural at-home event creates a concrete local problem. The residents are competent people, not passive recipients. The Bell family listens, tries, fails, adapts and helps them achieve an earned ending. At least one unfamiliar visual/world rule produces a consequential, logical surprise. Not every location must be a newly invented world: returning to Underleaf can reveal a new social group or a changed rule.

**C. Veylora engine (across arcs):** Evidence about missing people, bell routes and sabotage evolves slowly. A local story can reveal a verified trace, refute a false lead, change a relationship or establish a useful ally. NO mandatory villain teaser every episode and no magical clue appearing without causes.

A 45-minute episode is ONE causally continuous film-like story by default, not an anthology of unrelated mini-stories. The S09 anthology skill remains disabled unless explicitly approved.

## 5. Proposed main mystery — a layered solution, not endless empty teasing
The Night of the Silent Bells has THREE linked questions, deliberately separated:
1. **What exactly broke?** The physical anchors, route registers and permission/signature system have distinct roles. Early evidence proves failures but not motive.
2. **Who profited from the break?** A network operator, a faction, or a hidden broker may now control safe destinations and resettlement records. The recurring unknown adversary is tentatively called **"the Quiet Keeper"** — a title/hypothesis, not a locked identity.
3. **What would full restoration cost?** Some routes may have been unsafe or coercive even before the sabotage. Merely rebuilding old centralized control might repeat the original harm.

**PROPOSED TWIST LADDER (for editorial review; NONE CANON-LOCKED):**
- T1 (episodes 1–100): matching odd notches/station labels establish a real pattern but do not establish the saboteur.
- T2 (101–200): original records reveal several intentional route closures on the same night. A simple "accident" explanation is no longer plausible.
- T3 (201–300): a reliable witness contradicts Orin's early reconstruction, damaging family trust without making Orin secretly evil.
- T4 (301–400): the supposed Quiet Keeper seems tied to a rescue, complicating villain assumptions; rescuing people and hiding records can coexist.
- T5 (401–500): a powerful institution benefited from everyone losing independent routes; a false public account emerges.
- T6 (501–600): the family finds proof that one part of the shutdown prevented a separate catastrophe, but the deliberate dispersal and forged records were still wrong.
- T7 (601–700): a missing family member's possible choice/path becomes the emotional question; "found" does not mean someone must return.
- T8 (701–800): the antagonist attempts a second network change. Local communities face a real choice between access and autonomy.
- T9 (801–900): a family member publicly admits an earlier error, allowing allies to act on correct evidence; several routes can be reopened under consent-based rules.
- T10 (901–1000): the conspiracy is proved, people and communities decide how routes should be rebuilt, an intimate family reunion/closure becomes possible, and the Bell family chooses a better future rather than merely restoring the past.

**Avoid deterministic spoilers:** identity of the final antagonist, maternal grandmother's fate, which family member returns, and Orin's exact knowledge are **OPEN DECISIONS**. The story must support substantive reveals and alternatives rather than writing 800 episodes to protect one secret.

## 6. World/bell rules that make problem-solving dramatic
- **Address + anchor + permission + return:** All four are needed for a safe approved crossing. No arbitrary portal rescue in the final minute.
- **Finite knowledge:** Bubu's encyclopedia can be incomplete. Bix's instruments measure only what is measurable. Orin is powerful, not omniscient.
- **Consequence:** Wrong routes can cause delays, separation, failed delivery or danger, not grotesque horror. Rules must be set up before payoff.
- **Agency:** Residents of each world make choices about their own homes and futures. Aila's freedom is a precedent, not an exception.
- **Truth ledger:** Candidate clue -> corroborated physical trace -> verified route fact -> verified identity -> genuine reunion. Never equate same name or similar symbol with a recovered relative.
- **Everyday integrity:** No spell shortcuts for ordinary school tests, relationships or fair competitions.
- **Secrecy:** Earth-relative knowledge must follow the resolved canon issue above, not writer convenience.
- **Visual authority:** Original 9 canonical faces/bodies; location registry; no new world asset declares itself approved. Screenplays may propose locations but production needs separate location/asset registration.

## 9. Proposed 45-minute episode architecture (not proof of runtime)
- 00:00–02:30: concrete visual cold-open surprise; immediate person and question.
- 02:30–11:00: establish local need and home pressure; protagonist selects an imperfect solution.
- 11:00–21:00: the first plan fails due to an established world rule; small humor under pressure.
- 21:00–32:00: a human relationship/ethical choice becomes more costly; genuine reversal.
- 32:00–41:30: protagonist changes method, works with locals, earns a visible solution.
- 41:30–45:00: aftermath at home; one correct fact or honest emotional consequence continues the series.

Default is 45m story, verified only through actual scene-level full screenplay + performed timed read. Dialogue is natural slow A2 with distinct voices, not narration; images and reactions carry hard ideas. We do NOT pre-assign exactly 270 shots until the film edit and speech timing are checked.
