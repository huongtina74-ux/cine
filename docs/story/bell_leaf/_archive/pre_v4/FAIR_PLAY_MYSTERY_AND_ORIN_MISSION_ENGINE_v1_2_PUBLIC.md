## Three A/B/C story engines — no monopoly of mission stories
A. **Verified Orin assignment:** survivor trace, record check, protection, materials trade, bell restoration research via already SAFE worlds. Verify genuine sender and Earth return path. Orin is elsewhere for another reason; he cannot fix every link himself.
B. **World- or home-origin surprise:** an emergency from an established ally, a parcel from an already connected relay, a strange local phenomenon, or the family volunteering to help a friend. An unknown world cannot send an arbitrary full-size portal through Earth with no two-way repair.
C. **Bellfern home and relationship:** Mara's duty to both worlds; Evan's work and trust; children’s school and friendships; the grandparents' ordinary lives; scenes about shared goals and making difficult choices. A return home is earned by tested routes, not a plot-curfew magic spell.

## Technical causality and clue integrity
- Distinguish world rumor, independently verified address, responding Root Bell, fixed link, tested two-way return and family-safe travel. These are NOT interchangeable statuses.
- The source of a mission must be testable; a forged paper seal may be authentic material but an invalid assignment. A family member can reject a suspicious mission and keep the plot engaging by investigating via a known safe route instead.
- The use of rare material requires a source, tradeoff and continuity record. Skilled bellmakers have specializations, own goals and incomplete knowledge. No orphaned convenient universal repair spell.
- Earth closure: historic unreliable interworld return signal + noninterference law; the proposed lost-expedition record is a mystery, not proven lore. Catastrophic one-way gate ≠ working return path. The Orin-built private workshop receiver is a local endpoint of the ONE Earth world address, not a second World Root Bell or public legal reopening.
- Clues must carry source, time, custody and alternative explanations; no accusation based solely on shared hardware marks, names or vague magical voices.
- Distinguish (i) deliberate initial sabotage, (ii) emergency protective sealing, (iii) present-day interference. These may have different actors, proofs and motives. **Do not identify those actors in public documents.**
- Characters may be emotionally wrong; physics should not change to rescue a twist. A rescue or friendship must be visibly earned.

## Audience hook structure
- An episode opens with a meaningful concrete question or visual surprise, not a random reveal.
- Set up a first plausible interpretation; a planted observable contradiction turns it into an earned twist, changing protagonist's next decision.
- Resolve the local problem within the episode (45-minute target), then reveal one specific confirmed advance, contradictory evidence, meaningful character change or future obligation.
- Variability across episodes: Orin assignment, rescue request, personal goal, Earth family mystery, community revisit, known route’s altered condition, technical bottleneck, or political consent dilemma. No repetition of same black-cloaked figure / mystery sound / unearned cliffhanger.
- Every ~10–20 episodes: test/confirm/refute a real clue; every ~40–60 episodes: real rescue, world community payoff, route change or ethical decision with lasting consequence. Long chronology grows in 10 proposed 100-episode sagas; do not withhold all answers to episode 999.

## Example 45-minute episode engine
00–04m: concrete local problem, a named person with stakes.
04–10m: choice, sender-verification, safe-route plan, immediate family tension.
10–20m: destination culture and first plan; seeded object/rule.
20–29m: failed first attempt, first reinterpretation of clues, risk or cost.
29–39m: meaningful character choice, local people exercise agency, action payoff.
39–43m: visible solution and durable evidence ledger outcome.
43–45m: safe Earth return if justified, home emotional consequence, one precise unanswered question.
