# 1. Easy audience-facing rule (A2)
**"One world has one special bell. A safe bell can open only a safe door."**

- Each world has its **Root Bell** (the unique world-key and receiving gate/anchor).
- Ordinary trained users can say the route spell and ring a pre-tuned **Travel Bell** (personal portable bell). The portable bell holds a finite validated set of world signatures, not one new physical bell invented for each family traveler.
- The destination Root Bell must exist and answer correctly; the route and safe receiving area must be tested and the return route must be ready. Mere name or spell never builds the network.
- Before the sabotage the Tower distributed working verified addresses widely, making travel easy; after the sabotage, the address registry and the two-way handshakes are damaged, so familiar magic behaves like a locked door.
- Bubu's small collar bell may detect familiar sound but is **NOT** a world travel bell. The home workshop Root/Travel apparatus plus Mara's approved travel bell is what moves family members.

# 2. The four obstacles that make restoration difficult, plausible and dramatic
**A. Route address was erased.** Orin needs proof of the original note/address through damaged Tower notebooks, surviving station plates, old delivery seals, witnesses, saved ring-tone fragments, and his few trained former assistants. Guessing a name or humming a note does not make a safe connection.

**B. Root Bell still exists but cannot answer reliably.** Its receiving link is silent. Orin uses a diagnostic signal through an existing relay only to ask for a *resonance response*, not to transport a living person. A valid answer establishes that a Root Bell exists, not that passage is safe. A physical site might already have an old bellkeeper to help.

**C. Route part is missing/damaged.** A bell needs special resonant metal, a properly forged core and an aligned anchor. These are scarce on Earth; Orin must obtain material from *already reachable worlds* through genuine local adventures, exchanges and favors. Orin and later expert bellmakers do the specialized forging. Juno/Bix can build holding clamps, lenses and nonmagical instruments but cannot casually make rare magic ore.

**D. Return/receiving gate may be unsafe or forbidden.** Orin runs a small safe load test at a protected site, checks echo and return address, checks local community permission/ethics, and only then logs that route as VERIFIED_TWO_WAY. The old Veylora gate is still sealed and its return response corrupted/absent; Earth has been restored into a tiny protected two-way *home anchor*, not opened as a public station.

**The chicken-and-egg solution (important):** Orin does NOT need to visit an inaccessible world to repair every route. Many intact Root Bells can be awakened by correcting the **existing relay route** from a currently accessible neighboring world after a remote diagnostic response and local receiving permission. Some worlds are physically unreachable until:
  (i) their stranded bellkeeper or local ally repairs the receiving side,
  (ii) Orin reaches a different neighboring world with a live relay, or
  (iii) a previously reached route enables delivery of a rare component to another known technician.
Some worlds cannot be opened for hundreds of episodes. NO scouting probe transports people through an unverified gate.

# 3. The repeatable 7-step discovery-restoration flow (world route state machine)
1. **HINT:** family receives an incomplete address on an old postcard, finds a seal, hears a poor bell recording, or meets a displaced refugee in a currently accessible world. An unknown name could be invented or forged.
2. **CROSS-CHECK:** Orin checks **two independent traces**: old world map + correctly dated artifact, testimony + diagnostic note, or mechanical plate + known bellmaker observation. A clue remains "hypothesis" if not confirmed.
3. **FIND REACHABLE RELAY:** determine a pathway from Earth -> an existing SAFE world -> an intact relay; cannot bypass every broken link. The route graph gradually grows.
4. **DIAGNOSE:** Orin checks distant Root Bell status, physical materials and possible inhabitants. Classify route: dormant/intact, damaged, destroyed, blocked-for-safety, or UNKNOWN.
5. **REPAIR:** Orin and/or specifically trained bellmaker spend limited rare material to repair an actual failure; a local ally's independent decisions matter. If not enough, record UNREACHABLE_REQUIRES_COMPONENT and leave.
6. **PROVE TWO-WAY:** protective receiving space, local consent, small nonliving test, stable round-trip, then tightly supervised first crossing. No tested return = NO family traveling.
7. **REGISTER:** Orin writes a durable, witnessed entry in **the Repaired Bell Ledger**: route ID, parent relay, world Root Bell mark, who performed repair, material spent, permissions, test date, Earth return reserve, evidence level. Mara copies ONLY APPROVED SAFE routes to her travel bell at the Earth workshop.

**Exact rule for restoring return home:** Once Orin has repaired and repeatedly tested a small **Earth Home Anchor** secretly installed in Mara's protected workshop, every permitted route used by Mara's family carries a confirmed two-way path back to that anchor. This does not open forbidden Earth to all witches: only bells with the private Earth return note can connect.

# 4. What Orin physically does and why he is absent
During the years after the catastrophe:
- **Year 0:** Earth is a one-way emergency arrival; Orin and Mara are stranded there. He cannot immediately go home or choose any other world.
- **Early years:** Orin recovers old tools/notes and obtains tiny pieces of resonant metal through plausible means or remaining debris from evacuation; establishes a working private Earth anchor after repeated failures. First remote diagnostic contact is NOT a usable path.
- **Later years:** first one or two verified adjacent worlds become reachable, then a few more through chained relays. Numbers should remain unspecified until showrunner ledger approves exactly how many. Orin can finally travel and return, but costs and route choice limit him.
- **Present EP001 onward:** Orin frequently travels for days across available relays, follows a time-sensitive ferry/witness/rare metal lead, helps stranded witches and looks for his wife. He can return to Earth when safe but often chooses a mission over a family event. Family reacts emotionally and retains agency.
- A few former assistants/experts may emerge over long arcs with incomplete specialties; every one must have their own acquisition route, knowledge scope and trust evidence.

**Story exploration sources (no omniscience):** Bell library fragments / map pages, recorded bell-tones (not automatically reliable), old mail/postmarks, physical forge marks, rescued survivors, consenting local animal stories (Lia), imperfect memory of Bubu, mechanical measurements by Juno/Bix, crafted diagnostic response, old safe ports, rare material traders and expert former subordinates.

# 5. A typical week in Mara's family — Earth is HOME, worlds are ADVENTURES
**Permitted traveling party:** Mara plus Juno, Lia, Bix and Bubu. Orin may join or operate a separate quest. Evan remains ordinary human postal worker and **unaware of magic** under stricter existing active Canon; his parents also ordinary and unaware. No using Evan as a portal pilot/repairman unless the showrunner explicitly resolves prior conflicting pilot manuscripts. Family keeps school, dinner, bedtime and everyday obligations credible.

**Weekday, after school (illustrative):**
- 15:30 Juno and Lia return from ordinary Earth school; Evan is doing postal deliveries.
- 15:45 Mara receives a verified message from a previously restored world: villagers need urgent help or a survivor recognizes a genuine route seal.
- 16:00 MARA checks three things before allowing a trip: destination marked SAFE_TWO_WAY; Earth Home Anchor active and a return-reserve ring remains; children have a same-day return margin.
- 16:10 family rings the protected Travel Bell and enters one already-approved Gate Address (Underleaf, a verified cloud port etc).
- 16:10–18:00 adventure and local climax; do not assume every interworld clock differs. Time passes approximately normally in the base show to avoid school/custody contradictions.
- 18:00 Mara calls them back before the nightly anchor safe window closes. They speak the Earth return phrase and ring the pre-tuned travel bell at an approved local return pad.
- 18:10 Mara, children, Bix and Bubu emerge in the hidden Bell workshop. They clean up, hide magical traces, and resume domestic dinner.
- 18:30 Evan returns / family dinner; story's emotional payoffs live here. Grandparents may visit without learning magic.

**Weekend:** family can undertake longer 4–8 hour adventures; schools and elders remain grounded. An occasional special episode can strand them for one night, but only if a real failed safety condition earned that event and their plan for loved ones is dramatized. NOT the usual pattern.

**Safety default:** Families never enter a world unless (a) two-way return tested, (b) protected return location physically available, (c) adequate reserve and window, (d) permission, and (e) an adult supervises. A magical emergency may unexpectedly jeopardize these safeguards in a dramatic episode; it is not a routine convenience.

**Why they can't just teleport to grandma:** Her destination unknown, route archives intentionally corrupted; even a rumored name is insufficient. Once a world is identified, the distant receiving link may still be destroyed. The family's search progresses through earned evidence and achievable routes only.

# 6. Two levels of travel and mystery — keeps series alive
**COMMON FAMILY ADVENTURE (majority of episodes):** Visit previously restored worlds, return to known communities, solve a local conflict, discover a clue, identify material or meet a displaced member. "New adventure" does not mean a newly reachable world every episode.

**RARE ROUTE BREAKTHROUGH (major arc):** Orin repairs one more verified gateway after several episodes' groundwork, components and testing. The newly reachable world has distinctive history, ecology, social rules and a person's choice. Each breakthrough is a celebration *and* can reveal the next bigger obstacle. The world graph expands slowly: not 1,000 world links in 1,000 episodes.

**World of the week structure 45m:**
- 00–04m: impossible but concrete problem (a bell rings underwater, a child’s mother's letter arrives without a sender, a village moves at sunset).
- 04–12m: verified route, entry and local people/goal.
- 12–23m: family’s first attempt fails from a visible rule; Juno's logic, Lia's empathy and Mara's authority disagree for reasons.
- 23–34m: emotion/ethical reversal, a real cost, choice to help rather than merely collect clue.
- 34–41m: local payoff earned with residents; a truthful piece of network information or relationship change.
- 41–45m: safe return to Bellfern and one home-emotional beat; a limited open question, not generic villain shadows.
Duration still must be proven with written full scene dialogue and timed read.

# 7. Illustrative EP “THE BRIDGE THAT RANG AT SUNSET” — story beats
- A letter from Pip in Underleaf reaches Earth through an already restored safe channel: a village bridge has begun ringing like a World Bell at sunset, and its night market may be swept away.
- Mara verifies Underleaf's EXISTING TWO-WAY route, warns Evan only that the family will return for dinner; Juno/Lia/Bix/Bubu go with her through a protective workshop gate.
- Juno believes the bridge is a machine needing reinforcement; Bix measures an old metal bracket and identifies a separate note pattern. The bridge is not a brand-new portal: its beam contains an **old discarded relay bell plate**, shaking in evening wind.
- Lia asks willing local creatures where the sound began; they reveal the sound changed only after someone brought a sealed box to the bridge. The villagers themselves have already protected their homes in practical ways.
- First attempt to remove the metal plate makes the structure unstable; Mara stops the rescue and hears the market families are unwilling to abandon a reunion meal. A local elder proposes a temporary detour.
- They redirect people safely, secure the bridge and unbolt the harmful plate together; now the bridge holds. Underleaf festival proceeds in a smaller square. The local story genuinely ends, with no need to open any new world.
- On the plate is a rare forge stamp also present in an old damaged Veylora notebook. Orin cannot declare a culprit; two unrelated shops used similar stamps. He can now seek an old bellmaker to identify the workshop.
- At ~18:10 the family returns via their verified Underleaf exit to the Earth Home Anchor. They wash muddy boots before Evan comes in with post and eat together. Lia tells the truth about a bridge without disclosing magical details, leaving Evan's unaware status intact.

**What the episode actually advances:** the safe Underleaf route remains open; repaired bridge and local trust; inventory: physical plate added; mystery: possible forge provenance hypothesis -> next investigation; children grow in team skill. No impossible new-world connection fabricated.

2. **Earth Home Anchor:** restored after YEARS; controlled private two-way return; must remain non-public and not enable free entry to Earth by all witches. Suggested as canonical mechanical explanation for regular family adventures.

4. **Evan awareness, Bix origin, pilot temporal placement:** unresolved inconsistencies between some pre-existing narrative drafts; do not mark solved.
5. **Exact number of currently reachable worlds** remains unknown (only a FEW). No unearned "three" assertion.
6. **No independent approvals or market claims:** direct competitor AV/research and A2 measured table read remain blocked/unverified.

**Professional rationale:** This creates a dependable 'leave home → meaningful adventure → safe return home' loop, with periodic earned exceptions. The return-home ritual is a unique emotional hook, not a convenience. Orin's slow world-link reconstruction is the **long arc engine** and his family's trips to the already verified worlds are the **weekly/episodic engine**. Their actions bring Orin hard evidence and scarce materials; Orin's repairs create the next avenues for their adventures. Two halves of one causal story.
