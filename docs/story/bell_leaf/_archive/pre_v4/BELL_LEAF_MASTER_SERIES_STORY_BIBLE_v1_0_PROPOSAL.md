# 1. PREMISE — WHAT AUDIENCES ACTUALLY FOLLOW
**Core English A2 logline:** "Long ago, someone broke the magic bells. Families were lost in many worlds. Now one family is trying to find them and bring them home."

**The dramatic promise:** Every bell opens a door, but not every door leads home. Each episode changes at least one person's life in one world, while the Bell family slowly uncovers who broke the doors, why the forbidden Earth gate opened and what really happened inside Veylora.

**The structural engine is THREE interlocked series, not just portal tourism:**
- **A — ORIN'S MISSIONS:** Orin, the clan head, sends verified tasks to Mara's family through the few reachable routes. He has duties elsewhere, lacks local Earth knowledge, lacks time and cannot fix all doors alone. Missions are a causal continuation of the last rescue, proof or research result.
- **B — UNEXPECTED WORLDS / HOME INTRUSIONS:** A child follows an unfamiliar bell sound; a local festival turns dangerous; a misplaced parcel or injured creature arrives from a verified safe route; somebody calls for help. These cannot conveniently generate direct new unverified full portals into Earth.
- **C — FAMILY CHOICES / SOCIAL LIVES:** Juno's school and inventions, Lia's willingness to listen, Mara/Evan trust, grandparents, Orin's promises, a guest making a home. Sometimes the family travels because of a personal promise, not because grandpa commands it.

**Episode emotional structure:** concrete local WANT → conflicting methods and a first failure → a costly new choice → earned visible payoff → a quiet Earth-family moment. A durable series clue is a bonus that is only meaningful if it changes the story. An episode without a villain teaser can still advance the Veylora arc by establishing a true friend, safe route, rescued person or character decision.

**Tone:** warm wonder, cinematic magic, clever mysteries, funny character-based failures, family bonds and genuine danger without cruel horror. Juno/Lia are never used as disposable risk takers; villagers in visited worlds have knowledge and agency. Natural A2 dialogue is easy to follow, but plot intelligence is not childish.

# 2. BACKSTORY: THE CAUSE–EFFECT HISTORY FROM VEYLORA TO EP001

## T−0: BEFORE THE NIGHT OF BROKEN BELLS
Veylora was a prosperous living witch home world, with homes, workshops, children, routes and family traditions. The Great Bell Tower synchronized thousands of **World Root Bells**, each with its own world address and secure destination. People could use tuned *Travel Bells* by saying a short spell and ringing; they did not need to know metallurgy or remote routing. The clan recognized **OPEN**, **LIMITED** and **FORBIDDEN** destinations. Orin was both clan chief and Bell Keeper, politically accountable for safety but not omniscient.

Rare bellmakers were trained in distinct specialties: route maps, bell alloy/core, resonance and return-anchor control. Even a chief could not instantly rebuild a continent-wide network alone.

Earth was already a **forbidden Silent World**: its public route had been removed from ordinary route maps after historical unexplained return failures; public magic there was additionally prohibited to protect ordinary human societies. A ban is NOT physical nonexistence. Some old forbidden archives preserve an Earth address. Nobody may assert that not a single witch had ever reached it, nor that Earth magic is completely impossible.

## T−0: THE DELIBERATE ATTACK
A person or network with privileged access tampered with the Tower route synchronizer, intentionally crosswiring World Bell address/return instructions. The attacker deliberately initiated the corruption; this was not a benevolent failed experiment. In a family-friendly spectacular catastrophe, portals open to the wrong climates/physics, impossible weather and gravity flood Veylora's streets. A protective boundary around the central crossing hub tears, creating **the Hollow Ring**: a widening pressure fracture that pulls travelers, houses and materials toward unstable openings.

Why is evacuation mandatory? People cannot simply remain in their homes: their streets are physically losing stable ground and uncontrolled doors are pulling them into other worlds. Orin orders an emergency evacuation rather than risking everyone.

## T−0: EVACUATION, TWO DIFFERENT ACTS
The emergency evacuation opens remaining safe-exit mechanisms, but corrupted routing makes many exits ONE WAY, changes their destination, and drags neighbors in divergent directions, often without their prepared travel bells. Family and clan scatter across worlds. Some routes stop responding while travelers are already on the far side.

**The DELIBERATE NETWORK SABOTAGE is an attack. The FINAL EMERGENCY ISOLATION is a separate decision to keep the disaster from spreading.** It may have been made by an ethical guardian; it is not evidence of benign original intent. Who made it and at what cost is withheld until supported by witnesses and records.

Grandmother is last seen helping other refugees, not confirmed dead, not confirmed alive, not visually introduced with an invented face. Orin and Mara are pulled through different emergency gates unexpectedly converging on Earth, a destination forbidden for generations. They must first find one another on Earth; even the arrival of father and daughter at the same planet is inexplicable. This is one of the central mysteries.

## T+0 TO T+YEARS: EXILE, NEW HOME AND THE FIRST SAFE LINK
Orin's burned personal travel bell and salvaged notes carry partial residual Earth address data and a few formerly verified destination keys. The public Veylora gateway remains sealed. Orin has no way home and only scarce magical material on Earth.

Orin and Mara initially struggle to survive and reunite on Earth; later Mara creates the concealed Bell workshop. She eventually meets the human postman Evan, and Juno and Lia are born. Juno builds Bix per the active Series Bible (do not silently switch his creator; an alternative draft giving Orin original Bix architecture is UNRESOLVED and requires a showrunner correction). Bubu is a purple bell spirit familiar to the family, not an all-knowing GPS.

Orin spends YEARS repairing a PRIVATE EARTH RECEIVING ENDPOINT paired with the single original Earth World Bell address; this is NOT a second Root Bell and does NOT lift Earth's public prohibition. With a surviving restricted point-to-point bypass from a once-verified world address, he begins with weak remote diagnostic signals. He finds a responsive Root Bell, uses nonliving trial objects, tests RETURN, and only then conducts a protected first crossing. He gathers more scarce material and supports a handful of further safe routes. The exact number of safe routes at EP001 is deliberately unspecified beyond "a few." No family crossing occurs before full safe return tests.

He can now travel via known safe relays and sometimes come back, but cannot reach an arbitrary world or Veylora. His leads often take days or weeks; he may miss a family dinner or Lia's performance while chasing a short-lived witness, creating an emotional cost. Mara and the kids use only validated travel routes and still come home to Earth in ordinary episodes.

## THE STORY PRESENT (EP001)
Juno is 13; Lia 11; Earth school and Bellfern family routine continue. Orin has a limited network and a private return endpoint in Mara's workshop. The family can now accept **specific safe missions**; their discoveries help Orin repair more connections. The witch clan is still dispersed. Grandmother's location and the saboteur are unknown. Strange evidence suggests interference may still be occurring on a repaired route.

One real question links all eras: *Why did an ancient FORBIDDEN Earth gate open exactly when the chief and his daughter were trying to save their clan?*

# 3. WORLD/BELL RULES — NO CONVENIENT MAGIC

1. **One unique World Bell address** per world. A Root Bell plus anchor/permission determines a door to that world; the forged portable Travel Bell carries only a finite signed set of already verified addresses. Authorized traveler says an A2-friendly destination phrase and rings. Ordinary humans can cross an already-open safe door when accompanied; they do not need to be bellsmiths.
2. **Public ban vs physical link:** Earth is an ethical/political forbidden destination plus historically unreliable exit. Orin's private authorized-family emergency return path is technologically possible but remains legally controversial. Its endpoint is a private receiver on Earth's original unique world address — NOT a public rebroadcasting network or a new World Root Bell.
3. **No magic teleport by desire.** An unknown world name, half a bell tone, an old letter or a distant echo cannot open a safe portal. Both destination and return need independent verification; proof of a person living there is not proof of a traversable route.
4. **Finite materials and specialist time.** Resonant bell metal is rare; seals and anchors can break; trained bellmakers are dispersed. Juno/Bix make measurement and mechanical tools, not magically manufacture scarce resonant ore.
5. **Safety reserve and permissions.** A confirmed Earth-return anchor reserve and receiving location are required before Mara can bring children. Orin's experts can remote-diagnose an intact Root Bell through an existing relay; actually repairing a destroyed remote end requires local help or a second physical approach.
6. **Route state machine:** RUMOR → WORLD VERIFIED → ROOT RESPONDS → ROUTE REPAIRED → TWO-WAY TESTED → SAFE FOR FAMILY; any failure = SUSPENDED or FORBIDDEN. Do not jump ahead without evidence.
7. **No omniscient witnesses.** Bubu may misremember, Orin's archive may be corrupted, villains can forge some symbols, Lia hears willing creatures rather than infallible testimony, and Evan's postal dates can be wrong. Independent source checking is part of drama.
8. **Clock coherence:** Base rule is ordinary shared time; no convenient large time dilation. School and work schedules constrain trips. The 1,000 episodes are not automatically 1,000 consecutive Earth days. Explicit age/time changes require approved continuity and art references; locked initial Juno 13/Lia 11 remain binding until showrunner updates.
9. **Travel contacts can fail.** Remote audio or signed missions travel only across existing active safe signal routes (not necessarily physically open); letters delivered by known relays may be delayed. Deep unreachable worlds cannot magically text the Earth workshop. A fake mission would require stolen signature/evidence, not a free bypass of all security.
10. **Veylora is a mystery, not assumed annihilated.** The physical return line remains sealed for many seasons; contact or images are not automatic passage.
11. **Respect locals.** Witch access does not override the residents' right to refuse a visit or control their world; OPEN/LIMITED/FORBIDDEN categories affect both plot and morality.

# 4. ORIN'S MISSION SYSTEM — NEW PRIMARY EPISODE ENGINE

**Why Orin assigns work rather than always accompanying them:** he is a clan leader, not an isolated adventurer; he is hunting his wife, searching for rare bellmakers and conducting costly route repairs elsewhere. The Bell family is a trusted complementary team. Mara knows safe magic and carries home authority; Evan understands letters, trust and human systems; Juno/Bix solve technical measurements; Lia/Bubu understand living beings and magical stories. Missions need local observation, patience and empathy as much as brute force.

## Six canonical sources of action
- **M1 — Orin's direct assignment:** a sealed letter, valid message through a tested relay, or a personal briefing; explicit destination, goal, danger and return plan.
- **M2 — Survivor/distress request:** a known ally asks for help; not automatically a direct Orin order.
- **M3 — Unexpected event at Earth workshop:** an already connected route sends a wrong parcel, harmless creature or small signal; do not grant unknown worlds free Earth entry.
- **M4 — Independent Bell family initiative:** a promise to Aila, curiosity about a nonmagical school clue, grandfather's ordinary craft, Juno/Lia's decisions.
- **M5 — Rescue or protection of established community:** revisiting an old world where people have grown and circumstances change.
- **M6 — Orin field scenes / cross-cut narrative:** a side storyline with Orin's difficult decision and limited knowledge, sparingly combined with family actions so this remains a family ensemble series.

For 1,000 episodes, vary source combinations and protagonist; never force each episode to start with a magical mail envelope or end with a three-note black shadow. A story can start with M2 and reveal that an older M1 was misdirected.

## Mission request template, shown as real plot, not bureaucracy
- **Sender/verification:** how do they know it is genuinely from Orin? Signed bell rhythm PLUS separately checkable real-world clue if sensitive.
- **Specific goal:** find a missing record, protect a resident, bring one safe component, check a rumor, escort a consenting traveler.
- **Why this family:** what ability/context makes them better positioned than a bell master? What is Orin doing meanwhile?
- **Permitted route and Earth return:** world already SAFE, tested return, remaining time, backup protocol. Any destination still unknown is RECONNAISSANCE VIA RELAY, not child travel.
- **A moral choice:** find material versus help people, obey Orin's order versus respect local refusal, leave on time versus solve a friend's crisis.
- **Evidence for return:** object, witness account, verified timestamp, honest inconclusive report. **An honest "we could not verify" can be a successful mission** if that prevents a dangerous mistake.
- **Cost/consequence:** material spent, a family promise strained, a route suspended, an ally's trust gained, an enemy learns about a search.

## Examples of Orin's missions (not duplicated episode formulas)
**O01 — Find the Keeper's Seal:** Orin needs Mara to examine a mark on an old train platform within a SAFE world. It is not proof of a culprit; Evan later notices the stamp date predates the supposed closure.
**O02 — Bring a Rare Core Fragment:** market residents will trade material only if family helps repair a public water wheel, giving a satisfying local story and delaying Orin's next repair.
**O03 — Deliver the Letter, Not the Person:** the destination is safe but the addressee chose to remain there; the moral victory is delivering news without forcing a reunion.
**O04 — Test a Witness:** several survivors recall different gate colors; Lia hears one local bird's reliable location memory but must verify the time before accusing anyone.
**O05 — Protect a Bellsmith:** one of Orin's trained aides is alive but refuses to reopen a harmful world route. Mara must decide whether an order gives a right to compel them.
**O06 — Track the Wrong Delivery:** a parcel ostensibly from Orin's workshop arrived with a forged seal; Evan discovers a postal detail impossible for the claimed sender.
**O07 — Keep Earth Anchor Secret:** a suspicious visitor knows the original banned world symbol; Earth becomes an actual mystery location rather than a purely convenient home.
**O08 — No Mission Today:** Orin sends a peaceful note; Lia chooses to keep a promise to a Underleaf friend, and the good deed unexpectedly adds an independent witness.
**O09 — Rescue Without a Door:** a survivor is heard through a broken signal, but no tested portal exists. Orin forbids crossing; they must send instructions to a local bellmaker at the safe neighboring world. Emotion comes from delayed rescue, not magical loopholes.
**O10 — Family Dinner:** Orin can return but must choose between fleeting ferry proof and a long-promised family event. He makes a promise, sometimes breaks it, and earns consequences.

# 5. THE BELL FAMILY'S CHARACTER ARCS (WANT → FLAW → NECESSARY CHOICE)
**ORIN — the leader who could not bring his people home.** Wants to repair the network, protect scattered clan, find his wife. Flaw: treats his own sacrifice and secrecy as sufficient payment; tries to solve everything alone. Needs to delegate, disclose uncertainty, trust a nonmagical son-in-law, and face whether his own system created opportunities for a saboteur. He is not secretly guilty simply to manufacture a twist.

**MARA — protector who confuses love with hiding danger.** Wants family safety and answers about her mother. Her secrecy with Evan has a genuine cost and is not excused by good intentions. Needs to let every family member consent to their risk; she is the actual daily expedition leader, not merely Orin's assistant.

**EVAN — ordinary postman with an extraordinary family.** Nonmagical, observant, dependable, insecure about being excluded. Not a filler character: his independent postal knowledge finds false destinations and difficult truths no spell can reveal. **Proposed visible reveal EP002**: he finds out about magic rather than having known all along. He is hurt, helps avert family danger, and enters some later safe world missions after choosing it. The active Canon still says UNAWARE until this change is approved and shown. His human parents still unaware. His arc is earned trust and being a true equal, not secretly obtaining inherited superpowers.

**JUNO (13) — inventor, not universal bellmaker.** Wants to rebuild what adults cannot. Pride means his first mechanical answers sometimes fail; he learns to consult locals, share credit and test consequences. Bix is his actual helper/creation according to current active Series Bible; the alternate Orin-origin draft remains unresolved.

**LIA (11) — empathy without omniscience.** Wants every lost person found quickly, especially grandmother. Her hope causes mistaken identifications; listening to consenting creatures and apologizing when wrong become her courage. Learns that rescuing someone means respecting where they choose to live.

**BIX — measuring is not understanding.** Maintains equipment and test logs; can give precise but incomplete answers. He does not possess a magic universal bell or conjure rare materials. Learns when to hold back and let another person speak.

**BUBU — charm, humor and fallible lore.** Purple cat-spirit with a glittering spiraling tail, one collar bell; jokes, knows songs and forgotten world customs but sometimes misremembers. Wants to be indispensable; learns to admit "I don't know." Collar bell is not unrestricted interworld transport.

**GRANDPA_EARTH / GRANDMA_EARTH — everyday warmth.** Unaware of witch secret unless separate later on-screen approvals. Their ordinary skills and visits create real pressure and comfort. They are not recurring dupes or one-note obstacles. Evan must not treat them as disposable.

**MISSING GRANDMOTHER — person, not prize.** Mara's mother and Orin's wife had independent choices, loyalties and responsibilities during evacuation. Her disappearance is not proof of death or proof she is simply waiting to be fetched. Her identity/face and actual fate require separate approval; writers must not automatically replace/assign a new source image.

**AILA — rescued person with agency.** EP001 has Orin find Aila, give her a LIMITED pre-tuned personal bell and go after a time-sensitive ferry lead; her flying suitcase and chosen safe home remain. Later Aila's address and movement are her own choices; she isn't permanently assigned to Bell's house just for exposition.

# 6. THE HIDDEN MYSTERY AS A FAIR-PLAY CHAIN, NOT RANDOM TWISTS

## Six causal mysteries
1. **Catastrophe:** What technical intervention caused dangerous gate crosswiring?
2. **Forced scattering:** Why were emergency exits assigned unevenly? What role did original protections play?
3. **Earth paradox:** Why did a formally forbidden sealed world open, apparently delivering Orin and Mara?
4. **Missing family:** Where did grandmother and other witches go, and did some choose to stay and help others?
5. **Present interference:** Who continues modifying repaired relays and sending misleading clues?
6. **Control:** Why would anyone need to exile the only leader capable of repairing the network, and what power would return if the network came back under one authority?

## Writer's desired answer — role-based, NOT approved identity
The leading showrunner theory is **a conspiracy to monopolize the interworld registry and control who may cross**, organized by someone with ACCESS to restricted maps, and implemented by at least one person with physical bell-tuning skills. The saboteur caused catastrophe deliberately and planned to remove Orin from authority by routing him to the forbidden Silent World. During collapse, a *different* defender chose to isolate Veylora to prevent the spreading disaster. The principal attacker did not necessarily predict every result of that defense.

**This is a proposed coherent solution, not a confirmed character/culprit**. Do not introduce a named master villain or claim grandmother operated the defense until the showrunner reviews a private culprit and evidence dossier. The older "compassionate false-echo accident caused everything" proposal was superseded by user-confirmed DELIBERATE SABOTAGE.

## Evidence / misdirection promise
| Early clue | Audience's reasonable first theory | How a later FAIR reveal changes it | Local payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| A pre-disaster Earth symbol appears on restricted metal | Earth arrival was random | Someone deliberately preserved/opened an exiled address | Earth mystery deepens without instant identity |
| A bellsmith's forged mark occurs on a bad relay | Maker is the saboteur | Stamp was stolen or used by many; identity needs physical proof | Clears an innocent craftsman |
| Orin's own signature appears on a shutdown order | Orin caused catastrophe | Signature authorized emergency defense AFTER malicious crosswiring | Orin admits he misread his duty/history |
| Messages about grandmother have contradictory dates | A traveler is lying | The route timestamps were deliberately overwritten, or witness confused | Verifiable chronology replaces assumption |
| A lost witch refuses to go home | Person is being controlled | Person has an independent chosen family/home | Genuine autonomy; useful ally |
| An old safe world begins reporting identical false addresses | Technology spontaneously breaks again | Coordinated active interference uses known vulnerable relays | Present antagonist acts, not only distant evil |
| An Earth workshop scrap matches ancient forge metal | Earth once hosted the saboteur | Could also come from emergency travelers | Evan identifies postmark/fabrication date before accusation |
| Emergency isolation saved lives | Original attack was justified | Rescue and sabotage were separate acts by actors with competing aims | Honest moral distinction, no cheap redemption |

**Golden twist rule:** Plant observable evidence FIRST; at the reveal, an earlier scene gains a second coherent meaning. Never use "it was all a dream," convenient amnesia, random new powers, a secret villain twin or an unearned sudden resurrection.

## Seven twist types, rotated for 1,000 episodes
- **Goal reversal:** The item Orin needs would damage a local village; team chooses a harder solution.
- **Identity reversal:** Same name refers to a different person, and the mistaken assumption is shown as a mistake.
- **Helper reversal:** A suspect whom residents fear turns out to protect the dangerous gate, for a verifiable reason.
- **Rules reversal:** A world rule first appears magical but is mechanical/social; clues were visible.
- **Cost reversal:** Successful route repair consumes the family's only reserve, delaying another rescue.
- **Value reversal:** A missing witch wants contact, not extraction; the family respects the choice.
- **Conspiracy reversal:** A genuine protector's closing action was blamed for the original attack; later evidence separates the two.

Each episode needs no more than one major earned twist and perhaps a small emotional reversal; three empty shocks are worse than one strong payoff. A mystery reveal must NEVER depend on footage/data characters could not have known but audience was told it existed.

# 7. STORY STRUCTURE FOR A 45-MINUTE FILM-LIKE EPISODE (NOT FULL SCREENPLAY)
00:00–02:30 meaningful cold-open anomaly/need.
02:30–09:00 today's choice: Orin mission, surprise, or personal pledge; verify safe route and immediate emotional stakes.
09:00–18:00 arrive and meet local world; first plan seems plausible.
18:00–27:00 the plan fails BECAUSE of a planted rule/flaw, not random magic.
27:00–36:00 ethical/family conflict sharpens; protagonist must change method or sacrifice something.
36:00–42:00 payoff and local resolution; a precise new fact or changed relationship affects the series ledger.
42:00–45:00 verified return to Earth, family warmth or hard choice, a resonant but not compulsory question.
No repetitive anthropomorphic lecture; learning occurs through actions and dialogue. Actual complete Fountain screenplay, table read and A2 comprehension tests are still required.
