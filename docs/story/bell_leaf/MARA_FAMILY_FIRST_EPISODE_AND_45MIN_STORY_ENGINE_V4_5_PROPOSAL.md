## Showrunner's Series Format, Old/New World Travel and 40–50-minute Episode Engine v4.5
**Date:** 2026-11-06
**Status:** USER-DIRECTED EDITORIAL PRIORITY on story-development branch. NOT yet approved Canon Lock in active main. No 40–50-minute full screenplay, dialogue read-through, rendered episodes or YouTube audience data are claimed.

## 0. The showrunner decision in one sentence
**Mara, Evan, Juno, Lia, Bix and Bubu must be the PRIMARY POV for the overwhelming majority of the 1,000 episodes, primarily on NEW/UNKNOWN worlds that Bubu discovers.** Orin primarily travels OLD mapped worlds repairing routes and searching for ALL Veylora refugees; Mara family goes to OLD worlds only exceptionally when Orin gives a specific needed mission or a credible unexpected personal/urgent event occurs. Orin is a rare-on-screen grandfather, investigator and occasional event guest, NOT a parallel lead.

The series promises:
- Every episode is a complete compelling 40–50m magical-family adventure, with an independent local goal and satisfying changed-world payoff.
- Every episode offers a genuine fresh unexpected event: new world, surprising new district of known world, living magical phenomenon at home, lost-person mystery, ethical choice, or earned antagonist twist.
- The family evolves through humor, affection, courage, mistakes and reconciliations; every character has useful differentiated agency.
- Long threads gradually answer Veylora's Living Current decline, the catastrophe, the dispersed PEOPLE of the whole homeland, family members and the active recurrent antagonist—not postpone all answers until the end.
- Scripts aim natural accessible A2 English (no narrator) without lowering plot maturity; 40–50 minutes requires real dramatic beats, not repeated spoken explanations or endless scenic camera moves.

## 1. Travel mechanics — solve the "why can Mara visit old worlds from NEW Earth?" issue
**Before disaster:** At a mapped existing world's FIXED Gate Bell station, eligible passengers speak the proper destination incantation and ring THE STATION'S bell. Travel bells are NOT required per person. These historic old-world gate stations are *mapped*, but after disaster some are wrecked/isolated. Orin does professional repair; he cannot automatically travel all old routes.
**Earth during arrival:** Earth was genuinely NOT catalogued, so had NO old registered World Gate Bell. Orin/Mara landed through uncontrolled UNKNOWN fractures; an ancient "forbidden Earth bell" from obsolete v3 does NOT exist.
**Earth later (pilot-era, years after arrival):** Orin and Mara use recovered authentic bell cores, materials and testing to CREATE AND TUNE a **NEW physical Earth Workshop Gate Station**, gradually pairing it with an INITIAL SMALL NUMBER of existing repaired old-world stations. This is NOT a previously hidden Earth gateway and NOT an all-world network. The new station is maintained under Mara's workshop in Bellfern, distinct from Bubu's separately prepared **EARTH HOME ANCHOR** for bounded living-return magic. Ordinary Earth↔known-world gate travel requires a verified paired response, landing pad, authority and safe way back; each new route must earn its repair, evidence, and eventual local permission.
**At old world:** Mara and companions travel through the workshop's post-arrival FIXED Gate Bell (when a given link is certified) to an old connected world and ring the FIXED bell at its own gate to return. Bubu may accompany as a family member but is NOT required to activate a functioning known-world gate.
**At new unknown world:** Bubu's ONE original golden collar bell responds to a SPECIFIC prior sound trace of the catastrophe/unknown ecosystem; opens a temporary door if sufficiently strong. To the family the destination seems unpredictable; to the writer every event has a planted causal resonance source. Explore cautiously before entry, never demand that children jump blind. Bubu's unique home-note can return a SMALL physically present group to the separate Earth anchor when awake, sufficiently rested and the anchor itself works. Bubu does not pick arbitrary new worlds, create permanent Gate Bells or carry a civilization through one opening.
**Family safe return if an old gate breaks:** the family MAY have a small Bubu Earth fallback under actual cost/space/signal conditions; any old-world refugee community still needs large, repaired ordinary gateways for mass relocation. An old-world gate failure can still be serious when Bubu is exhausted/separated or the Earth home anchor is compromised.
**Orin's travel:** he works on the repaired old network, searching missing civilians, wife and sons and Veylora's path; he can arrive in an old world the family later visits. He does NOT own the team's mission choices. Orin can send limited real-path voice signals via Bubu, send surviving station letters, or disappear from the local episode entirely without an implausible reset.
**Veylora:** still unreachable until evidence, physical gate repair, and actual safe passage. The antagonist cannot teleport to Earth or Bubu-only unknown worlds without earning a route.

## 2. Character POV / screen-time target
**MARA:** default adventure leader, researcher and parent; central choice is often hers. Her ability to refuse Orin's dangerous mission is important.
**EVAN:** human postman/father and ordinary-life mystery solver; after a proper owner-approved on-screen magic DISCLOSURE, sometimes travels through already-open fixed gates or a safely tested Bubu portal with family. Other episodes he stays on Earth handling meaningful home/letter/human relations, never every episode as a tagalong. Active main Canon still starts with Evan UNAWARE; do not silently retcon.
**JUNO:** 13-year-old inventor, Bix's canon creator; identifies mechanical patterns, makes safe small probes, gets tempted to fix everything by force.
**LIA:** 11, empathy with willing animals, emotional trust and humane questioning; cannot summon all missing relatives.
**BIX:** compact turtle-pangolin robot, no bell; measurements/factual verification, comic confidence and learning limits; must not become all-powerful scanner.
**BUBU:** lovable sparkly purple bell-spirit cat with ONE collar bell; echo-driven unknown portals, limited return Earth home-note, Orin signal communication on reachable paths, and incomplete magical memory sensing. Own fear and decisions; not a remote control device.
**ORIN:** beloved clan-chief and occasional event guest. Illustrative slate of 20 episodes: present in person in about 1–2, heard via message/voice in about 3–4, absent on-screen from the REST, as editorial STARTING POINT only. He may feature in a dramatic special or a multi-episode reunion arc; do not erase his seriousness/own obligations. Family MARA still primary active protagonists.
**UNRELATED SURVIVORS:** all of Veylora, not just Elara, Rowan and Cael; stand-alone refugees and communities have their own interests and lasting outcome.
**ANTAGONIST:** Ash Bellkeeper sometimes appears in old-known worlds accessible to him and sometimes only influences a clue; strong scenes spaced with genuine victories/losses, not repetitive masked chase filler.

## 3. NEW RULE v4.7: Orin old worlds, Mara/Bubu unknown worlds — NOT equal ownership
**World geography editorial hypothesis for 20-episode planning (NOT measured audience preference):**
- ~65% NEW/UNKNOWN worlds, comprising Bubu's **first discovery** and meaningful revisits to those discovered worlds, with changing residents and real local outcomes.
- ~20% EARTH/Bellfern: family life, magical anomalies, care of rescued people, magic restoration experiments.
- ~10% OLD already mapped worlds: Mara's family enters ONLY for justified urgent Orin assignments, rescue of someone personally important, or unforeseen event affecting them; actual Gate pair must work.
- ~5% MULTI-location specials that naturally require interacting worlds.
**Orin's independent old-world investigations are normally OFF-SCREEN.** Brief proof-based messages/notes via Bubu when path exists, sometimes rare guest appearance. Do not label every Mara episode an Orin dispatch.
**Episode SUBJECT** is a second overlapping axis: discovered survivor, mystery, villain evidence, home comedy, family promise, ecological study, adventure, reunion; one doesn't imply a particular world's geography.
These percentages replace earlier 35% OLD, 30% NEW, 25% EARTH, 10% mixed. A NEW-world emphasis does not mean a brand-new world EACH episode; revisit worlds originally found by Bubu, especially to show how their communities change. The 46-minute Underleaf example below is an **EXCEPTIONAL old-world mission**, not the regular weekly format.

## 4. Seven types of opening — avoid same episode formula
1. **Home intrusion:** object/animal/call/bell sound turns an ordinary day strange; no assigned mission.
2. **Old-world known ally calls:** local person the audience already loves is in a NEW predicament.
3. **Bubu echo:** surprising phenomenon hints at uncharted world, but destination may not be entered in this episode.
4. **Lost-person proof:** a named person is confirmed/cleared or a group asks for connection, prompting humane local adventure.
5. **Family relationship consequence:** a child's birthday promise, Evan's work, Mara's secrecy, Orin missed dinner, grandparents' visit.
6. **Villain action:** on-screen danger near a REAL reachable Gate or evidence that someone is manipulating the old network; rare and consequential.
7. **Orin task:** a precise compelling ask, with why he cannot come and what Mara can actually decide. Use sometimes, not every episode.

No universal cold-open "Grandpa Orin calls Bubu, sends task"; no 10-minute exposition before anything happens. Hooks can be comedic, magical, mysterious or emotional, not always violence.

## 5. 40–50-minute SCREENPLAY standard: a 46-minute test format
**0:00–0:45 COLD OPEN (hook):** immediate unusual/specific event with emotional or physical stakes. Deliver the premise seen in title/thumbnail.
**0:45–06:30 ACT I (goal & bond):** introduce who's personally affected and precise answer needed; home-family humor grows from character, not random sketches.
**06:30–14:00 ACT II (exploration/action):** arrival and world rule/cultural discovery by physical experience, family starts a plausible plan.
**14:00–23:00 ACT III (first twist):** their initial interpretation is wrong due to *planted* evidence; a real decision raises stakes.
**23:00–32:00 ACT IV (real complication):** problem changes character relationship or consequence, not repetitive failed attempts; second earned reversal.
**32:00–41:00 ACT V (choice, cost, payoff):** family and locals act with sacrifice/limited resources and solve the local objective honestly.
**41:00–46:00 ACT VI (aftermath, emotional closure + serial thread):** changed relationships/community, return safely where physically feasible; optionally one proportionate clue/reveal to the broader mystery, not a mandatory cliffhanger.
Pacing grid is an editorial prototype; actual duration requires a complete scripted staged dialogue, action, beat-by-beat timing, A2 comprehension test and review. The target is NOT 46 minutes of continuous speech; physical performance, cinematic storytelling, reaction and visual wonder have meaning, not empty padding.

**Required beats per full episode (without overusing twist):**
- One **hook** with clear promise, not an invented scene that never develops.
- A goal + credible antagonist or obstacle (nature, social customs, misunderstanding, fear, villain or family limitation).
- One unique world mechanic/living environment element *used as plot*, not decoration.
- One emotional bond that makes the event matter to the family/local resident.
- One major reversal the audience can reconstruct from earlier information; optional second reversal only if it changes action meaningfully.
- One actual choice by Mara or an episode's chosen family protagonist with a cost or trade-off.
- A truly resolved local plot and cause-effect consequence on return.
- Clue/family/antagonist advancement optional in SOME episodes, not shoehorned into all.
- No sudden new powers, convenience orphan relative or deus ex Bubu.

## 6. Concrete 46-min OLD-WORLD episode treatment (later season, after Evan knows)
### Working title: "THE BRIDGE THAT TURNED TO GLASS" — Underleaf
**Primary team:** MARA, EVAN, JUNO, LIA, BIX, BUBU. Orin DOES NOT PHYSICALLY APPEAR. Secondary returning locals: Underleaf bellsmith Tali, school helper Mina and local council (all new proposed/supporting, require visual approval).
**Logline:** The great root bridge of a familiar forest village turns clear and brittle just as local children cross it. Villagers accuse a Veylora refugee who repaired a nearby station, but Mara discovers their successful "safety upgrade" is slowly draining living roots. To save the bridge, she must switch off the ONLY certified gate connection to Earth and choose a risky bounded Bubu trip home for her own family, while leaving the larger village no easy exit until the engineers make a sustainable repair.

**0:00–00:45 HOOK:** In Underleaf a root bridge that was strong yesterday turns glasslike as children run across it. The roots creak, and a bell rings from underneath, though no one touches it. An adult catches one child; the bridge is still unstable. "Why is the bridge breaking?"
**00:45–06:30 EARTH HOME:** Evan, now appropriately aware of magic, receives a verified urgent message from Underleaf school helper Mina through the newly BUILT Earth workshop Gate Station. Juno wants to rush in; Mara checks green paired route and decides safe accompanied visit. Bubu's collar flickers at an oddly regular second note. Evan reminds Juno about visiting the ordinary Earth grandparents later; ordinary family life gives time/cost stakes.
**06:30–14:00 UNDERLEAF ARRIVAL:** Mara's team rings the physical GATE Bell and enters a verified old station. Village kids wait far from the bridge. Council blames Veylora refugee bellsmith Tali: "The bridge changed after Tali repaired our gate." Mara speaks to both sides. Lia listens to a willing beetle that noticed water retreating. Juno and Bix inspect bridge roots with nonmagical probes; they seem dried from below rather than cracked from above.
**14:00–23:00 FIRST TWIST:** Tali intentionally restricted flow near the old Gate, and is accused of sabotage. Evan finds station maintenance notes and hears Tali's reason: gate machinery has begun pulling living root moisture/energy after a new "safety regulator" was installed. The council itself voted for the regulator after an earlier accident. There is no monster or secret enemy physically at Underleaf needed for this episode's main conflict. Bubu hears the regulator pulse in a second unnatural rhythm, but cannot identify its maker.
**23:00–32:00 SECOND TWIST / ETHICAL CRISIS:** Stopping the drain means shutting down the only presently certified Underleaf↔Earth fixed bell route until it can be rechecked. The local council refuses at first because it needs contact with other worlds and evacuation routes. Juno discovers partial irrigation water is being diverted into the same powered mechanism, and the critical bridge needs fresh water NOW. Mara asks people to choose children/forest over convenience and discloses the real travel consequence for her own family.
**32:00–41:00 ACTION & REAL PAYOFF:** School kids moved to safe banks; Lia works with local animals and a school helper to locate original feeder channels, Juno/Bix isolate the regulator and erect a NONMAGICAL temporary water guide, Tali and Mara manage the gate shutdown safely. Underleaf villagers provide human labor and tools. The roots gradually take water and become flexible, preventing full collapse. The bridge is temporarily stabilized for restricted use, NOT instantly magically reborn. Council publicly clears Tali; volunteers plan an eco-safe future station refit.
**41:00–46:00 EMOTIONAL ENDING + EARNED CLUE:** Underleaf apologizes to the Veylora survivor. Children thank Mara, whose family misses the Earth grandparents' arranged dinner because of rescue. The fixed gate remains OFF pending tests; Bubu, who is present and rested and has an unobstructed Earth anchor, safely opens ONE bounded home-return for the six family members, not for the whole village. At home Evan finds the regulator's paper manifest dated AFTER the supposed installation approval: a reproducible clue to falsified supply documentation. The family agrees to investigate who authorized it, but today's town is safe. Orin need not call and explain everything. Episode stands alone as responsible rescue and family drama.
**A2 sample:** "The roots are dry." / "The gate is taking their water." / "Can we save the bridge?" / "Yes. But we must close the gate first." / "Then we will find a different way home."
**Twist fairness:** altered root pattern, beetle testimony and new regulator are planted before accusations flip. The regulator is a recurring sabotage research clue, but the accused refugee isn't a secret bad guy. Bubu return is earned by pre-established powers and valid family+anchor conditions.

## 7. Four-year / 1,000-episode fatigue prevention (not an automatic formula)
**Rotate PRIMARY POV:** Mara default; episodes can pivot to Evan postal detective, Juno and Bix invention mistake, Lia and Bubu friendship, Mara/Evan marriage, a rescued local friend. Orin rare specials; no family character reduced to one repetitive gimmick.
**Rotate STORY EMOTION:** wonder/adventure, comedy, detective, gentle fear, family reconciliation, mystery, rescue, social conflict, festival, creative survival. A2 vocabulary simple but stakes/surprises genuine.
**Rotate GATE START:** not always "Bubu rings once; portal opens." Family can start on Earth or revisit known friends; the signature portal entrance is a reward when appropriate.
**Rotate TWIST:** "suspected villain was helper," "the lost person chose to stay," "signal was old, not live," "a beautiful place is in ecological danger," "a puzzle has two legitimate answers," "hero's tool hurt the world," "a friend seems to lie to protect someone," "Bubu hears a different meaning." Clues appear BEFORE payoff.
**Rotate OUTCOME:** a friend/contact persists, a known city changes, a route shuts as cost, a new gate opens after many steps, an emotional promise comes due, a refugee chooses home. Avoid status reset.
**Reveal discipline:** answer one small real question every ~3–5 episodes, one substantial state change per 12–20, occasional major act break per 25–40. These are production hypotheses; compare with channel data instead of asserting fixed psychological law.
**World asset realism:** one-off new world every episode would require ~1,000 world environments and huge animation burden. Introduce new worlds deliberately (pilot test 2–3 actual first arrivals per 20 episodes), and revisit rich mapped and newly-discovered worlds with distinct villages, different residents and time consequences. Treat 1,000×45 minutes = ~750 hours, at FIXED 10 seconds/shot ~270,000 shots; achieving coherent complete storytelling will require pilots, approved reference sheets, independent QC and staged production rather than auto-writing all scripts at once. Ages: Juno 13/Lia 11 in active main; 1,000 episodes spanning years require a planned age/time canon, or a floating fictional timeline. Never silently change child faces/ages during production.
**YouTube validation:** official YouTube Analytics measures intro retention after first 30 sec plus top moments, spikes and dips; verify on actual published comparable-length Bell & Leaf episodes before selecting a definitive new-world/revisit ratio. A script's beat template does NOT guarantee 40-min retention.

## 8. Production "NO-GO" checks
- FAIL if Orin is the primary explorer of all old worlds while Mara's family only waits for random Bubu doors.
- FAIL if Bubu can arbitrarily select any realm, transport crowds, find family by telepathy, or ignores home anchor and stamina.
- FAIL if Earth already had an old forbidden gate, or everyone requires a personal travel bell.
- FAIL if mapped Gate Stations don't need real pairing/repair, or unknown temporary portal automatically becomes a permanent ordinary route.
- FAIL if every episode opens with Orin calling or ends with Isel/Ash Bellkeeper shadow.
- FAIL if a 45-minute episode contains just one obstacle stretched with redundant actions, empty dialogue, same reaction shots or continuous camera zooms.
- FAIL if local problem not solved, or 3 fake cliffhangers replace character decisions and evidence.
- FAIL if villain appears in a physically unreachable unknown realm without a real previously earned path.
- FAIL if Bubu loses sparkly spiral purple tail, gains multiple bells, or Bix receives a bell; new supplementary locals require source/ref approval.
- FAIL if ordinary Evan is magically informed before owner-approved on-screen reveal, or the scope of clan rescue shrinks only to Orin's direct relatives.
- FAIL if an episode is marked 45min, A2 PASS or Canon-approved without timed complete script, content test and source image references.

**ONE-LINE SERIES PRODUCTION CONTRACT: "The old world may be familiar, but the story must be new. The new world may be strange, but the family must remain the heart."**
