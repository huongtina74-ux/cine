## I. Why this show exists
**Logline (Vietnamese):** Mỗi ngày, một gia đình bình thường ở Bellfern bước qua chiếc chuông tới những nơi phi thường, giúp một người giải quyết điều tưởng không thể. Mỗi cuộc gặp gỡ đưa họ gần hơn tới sự thật về một đêm khiến hàng nghìn gia đình Veylora lạc mất đường về — và một câu hỏi đau lòng: tìm thấy nhau có đồng nghĩa phải trở về?

**A2-facing sentence:** "We have a home. Many people do not. We can help them find a safe way."

**Two audience promises:** (1) entertainment: magical novelty, visual clarity, funny character choices, a local satisfying ending and deep family bonds; (2) incidental learning: natural American-English A2 spoken in contextual action without narrator, vocabulary lectures or dead expository scenes. It remains family-friendly, but not mechanically simplified to target children only.

**Moral center:** A loving home is the place you are allowed to return to, not the place someone chooses for you. **Moral antagonist:** "Safety requires control. The map must belong to one keeper." The central conflict is between care and control, not magic vs no-magic.

**What this show is NOT:** not eleven disconnected short stories in one long video; not repeated tourist visits to beautiful worlds; not a lecture about grammar; not a family of omnipotent wizards; not a procedural where every episode is a lost parcel/gear; not 1,000 episodes protecting the identity of one villain as a single cliffhanger.

## II. Narrative foundations: the four simultaneous stories
1. **The emotional family story.** Children grow up, elders make choices, promises have a cost, Evan belongs even when he cannot cast spells, Mara cannot always protect everyone, and Orin has to choose when to return. Family dinners are both narrative punctuation and emotional pressure.
2. **The local adventure.** In an alien yet relatable world, local residents face a genuine person-level problem with immediate stakes: a bridge supports one community but blocks another; a village cannot agree how to share rain; a messenger must choose whom to help first. Residents own their solutions. A Bell family member changes through their own decision.
3. **The network investigation.** Clues are not magic coupons. A clue has a source, a time, a reliability level and a consequence. Even disproving a wrong lead moves someone forward emotionally or changes procedure.
4. **The ethical choice.** The safe, easy route may remove another person's agency. Aila's freedom in the pilot establishes this conflict. A person may be found but not wish to return to Veylora; a closed door may be protection rather than cruelty. Genuine perpetrators remain responsible for intentionally stranding people.

## III. The Night of the Silent Bells — history without fake locked answers
**Confirmed premise from existing Canon:** deliberate sabotage broke/scattered Veylora's bell network, separating witches/families. Some routes are safe and supervised. Orin investigates and may return to Earth, but does not always choose to leave a time-sensitive lead. No identity/motive for a saboteur has been approved.

**Working hypothesis — Option E, subject to approval:**
- A route system has three separately understandable physical functions: MAP (destination), HOME (safe return anchor), KEY (permission). These are dramatic objects, not terminology to dump in dialogue.
- An old emergency happened. One actor may have temporarily shut dangerous gates; separately, an opportunist may have changed registers or blocked selected routes for gain. This is **not yet Canon**.
- "The Quiet Keeper" is the name survivors whisper when they do not know whom to blame. It can be a role, rumor, framed protector or betrayer. The writers MUST NOT assign a single canonical human face until a showrunner decision.
- Early evidence can expose a changed route number or contradictory dated record, but NEVER claim a grandmother/aunt was located based solely on sound, family name or letter.
- Later revealed events must be traceable to pre-established maps, decisions and records. No resurrection, time reset, "it was all a dream," or conveniently omniscient magical object.

**Who benefits if the hypothesis is true?** Possible (not yet approved): `The Route Office` holds exclusive maps and insists strict control is necessary; `The Lantern Couriers` preserve open correspondence, but some may be unreliable; `The Shelter Circle` helps migrants choose homes even when the old network demands return. Keep institutions distinct from moral identity of individuals: a kind employee can work in a flawed system; a rescue volunteer may misread evidence. No faction is a Canon asset yet.

**Central outer stakes:** Not merely "will the family find Grandma?" but "will strangers and family regain the ability to choose where they live safely?"  
**Central intimate stakes:** "Will Lia and Juno still trust Mara and Orin if some rescue stories turn out to be incomplete?"

## IV. Non-negotiable bell physics — drama comes from limits
**R1 The Four-Part Safe Crossing:** Known destination + stable anchor + approved permission + verified return. The home bell and Orin's personal travel bell are real established objects; other bells are only as capable as their authorized route register permits. This is a **proposed formalization** of existing safety practices, not new magical technical Canon without signoff.

**R2 No omniscient search:** A bell does not locate a person merely by saying a name. Same-name records are not identities. A personal voice can be misunderstood; do not retroactively make Bubu's audio knowledge infallible.

**R3 A return has a cost:** Real-world travel time, a disappearing ferry, a temporary anchor window, fatigue or a local promise supplies constraints. Do not invent a universal mana meter or teleportation tax unless Canon approves it.

**R4 Ordinary skills remain valid:** Evan's letters/addresses, Grandma's cooking/social care and Grandpa's tools solve real problems. Spellcasting does not substitute for listening, skill, safe consent or honest speech.

**R5 Magic cannot cheaply remove emotion:** It cannot repair a broken promise, produce a parent's presence in two places simultaneously, force forgiveness or decide a refugee's home. The local resolution must come from character choices.

**R6 Visualizable limits:** Every new mechanism needs (a) one visible cue, (b) one spoken A2 line showing the rule through action, (c) a real consequence within the episode, (d) an earned callback payoff. Do not add unexplained portal powers in a climax.

**R7 New-world rigor:** Every proposed world needs one unique physical property, one social system, one local resident with agency, one limit/cost, one conflict unrelated to Veylora, one potential honest clue, and two reusable locations before asset production.

**R8 Learning rule:** No narrator. A2 dialogue uses short familiar connected sentences and conversational speech acts (ask, explain, compare, apologize, invite, decide, disagree); acting and visual causality can carry more complex ideas. A2 human listening test required; do not pretend a script's short sentences prove CEFR suitability.

## V. The nine character engines — desire, fear, flaw, and irreversible growth
### MARA — mother / magician, family anchor
- **WANT:** keep her children safe while helping find scattered relatives.
- **FEAR:** one child disappears as Veylora relatives did; she sees caution as responsibility.
- **FLAW:** treats loved ones as people to protect, not partners allowed to choose.
- **CONTRADICTION:** trusts strangers to choose homes (Aila) but sometimes restricts Juno/Lia because they are her children.
- **EARLY CHANGE (001–100):** authorize small supervised risks, confess when a clue is uncertain.
- **MIDDLE CHANGE (101–600):** discover her rules kept someone safe *and* made another person feel unheard.
- **LATE CHOICE (601–1000):** relinquish centralized control of a safer network; retain the freedom to close a dangerous route locally.
- **A2 voice:** calm, exact, kind, few words when afraid: "Show me what you know." "We can try. We must check first."

### EVAN — human father / postal worker
- **WANT:** be present and trusted when magic overshadows ordinary life.
- **FEAR:** that his family has a second life where he is unnecessary.
- **FLAW:** promises to keep everything normal and hides personal worry behind helpfulness.
- **CONTRADICTION:** reads strangers' addresses with great care, but hesitates to ask questions about his own family.
- **EARLY CHANGE:** uses postal verification to save a small delivery and help people choose a home; stands up for an ordinary dinner.
- **MIDDLE:** spots an alteration in mail/date records missed by magic-based investigators.
- **LATE:** helps design a voluntary address/privacy system and asks an honest question he previously avoided.
- **A2 voice:** grounded, gently funny: "A letter needs the right home." "Please tell me what happened."
- **CANON BLOCK:** unresolved whether Evan knows visible magic. Write only knowledge-neutral scenes until approved.

### JUNO — 13 / inventive maker
- **WANT:** build tools that repair what adults cannot.
- **FEAR:** that he will fail when someone depends on his invention.
- **FLAW:** mistakes complexity for progress; invents solutions before asking local users.
- **CONTRADICTION:** wants to be independent yet depends on Bix/Grandpa for feedback.
- **EARLY CHANGE:** fair school work, patient measurement, builds tools others can use.
- **MIDDLE:** discovers an impressive prototype that would allow dangerous centralized access and chooses not to deploy it.
- **LATE:** makes interoperable safe tools that communities can inspect and maintain independently.
- **A2 voice:** energetic practical curiosity: "Let me test it." "I think this part is too heavy."
- **CANON BLOCK:** Bix original creator (Juno vs Orin) not resolved; no origin-exposition until approval.

### LIA — 11 / animal empathy
- **WANT:** understand every creature, help every lost person, bring family back together.
- **FEAR:** someone she loves can leave and never return.
- **FLAW:** translates hope into certainty ("it must be her") and sometimes overhelps a creature that needs rest.
- **CONTRADICTION:** hears animals brilliantly but sometimes misses what humans say plainly.
- **EARLY CHANGE:** learns to observe a sleeping seed, apologizes after mistaken blame, learns person≠name.
- **MIDDLE:** learns that feeling a creature's fear doesn't prove who frightened it; asks what the person wants.
- **LATE:** permits a loved one to choose a different home and still keeps an honest relationship.
- **A2 voice:** observant emotionally clear: "It sounds sad." "What do you want?" "I was wrong."

### BIX — compact turtle-pangolin robot, no bell
- **WANT:** prevent errors by measuring everything.
- **FEAR:** causing harm through an untested mechanism.
- **FLAW:** equates a precise number with complete understanding.
- **CONTRADICTION:** protects Juno by doing too much of Juno's work.
- **EARLY CHANGE:** lets Juno/grandfather solve physical tasks; asks a local maker about materials.
- **MIDDLE:** admits when measured data cannot explain the reason for a person's choice.
- **LATE:** designs tools that reveal uncertainty rather than claiming certainty.
- **A2 voice:** compact, literal, unexpectedly dry: "The hole is too big." "We need another test."
- **Visual truth:** no chest bell, no bulky arbitrary redesign, approved original face/body.

### BUBU — purple bell spirit/cat, little sparkles in spiraling tail and one collar bell
- **WANT:** be a treasured guide whose knowledge seems amazing.
- **FEAR:** becoming useless if the old bell encyclopedia is wrong.
- **FLAW:** overconfident, too loud, eager to interpret before listening.
- **CONTRADICTION:** knows many words but not enough context, especially for unfamiliar residents.
- **EARLY CHANGE:** corrects the plant and signal pages, accepts Lia's better observation.
- **MIDDLE:** recognizes a famous bell signature can have multiple explanations.
- **LATE:** publicly admits a big failure and helps others build a shared correction system.
- **A2 voice:** warm theatrical humor, short accessible lines: "Wonderful! Wait. Maybe not." 
- **Visual truth:** sparkling curled purple tail in every appropriate scene.

### ORIN — Mara's father / powerful traveling wizard
- **WANT:** follow evacuation trails and reunite separated families.
- **FEAR:** once stopping will mean someone else is never found.
- **FLAW:** a mission can turn into an excuse to postpone ordinary emotional responsibility.
- **CONTRADICTION:** can travel home with his own bell but sometimes chooses a lead over a family promise.
- **EARLY CHANGE:** apologizes without defending every choice; establishes reliable two-way messages.
- **MIDDLE:** his chronology of the network's fall is honestly challenged; he verifies rather than claims he must be right.
- **LATE:** delegates; accepts that a true hero sometimes returns, listens and tells the incomplete truth.
- **A2 voice:** patient, sparse, emotionally weighted: "I know what I saw. I do not know why."

### GRANDPA_EARTH — human paternal grandfather, nearby
- **WANT:** remain a useful teacher/relative as the children grow.
- **FEAR:** becoming a visitor who can only watch.
- **FLAW:** assumes his old repair methods fit every problem.
- **ARC:** ordinary craft coexists with Juno's new thinking; accepts new ideas; no magical knowledge.
- **VOICE:** patient practical humor: "Try the simple thing first."
- **CANON:** never reveal magic accidentally without an explicit future showrunner-approved arc.

### GRANDMA_EARTH — human paternal grandmother, nearby
- **WANT:** keep the family connected through time, food and visits.
- **FEAR:** being politely excluded from family change.
- **FLAW:** sometimes assumes care means showing up before anyone asks.
- **ARC:** learns to respect privacy while offering presence; her ordinary empathy helps Mara and Lia in real crises.
- **VOICE:** teasing warmth with clear questions: "Do you want help, or do you want me to listen?"
- **CANON:** no magical knowledge.

## VIII. World identity and respect
No world exists solely to hand the Bell family a clue. Give local people jobs, culture, homes, humor and unresolved personal lives. A resident can solve an aspect the visitors cannot. A2 storytelling can convey cultural complexity through a meal, request, market, choice or apology instead of lore monologues. Do not treat relocation as an automatic happy ending; safe community and consent matter.

## IX. Evidence system — Never let speculation become Canon
**Clue categories** (reader-facing):
- `RUMOR`: one unconfirmed account; useful for asking questions.
- `PHYSICAL_TRACE`: genuine object/sound observed; its meaning is open.
- `CORROBORATED`: two independent observations support a factual detail.
- `ROUTE_VERIFIED`: a station/connection proved safe/real, not a person's presence.
- `IDENTITY_VERIFIED`: independently confirmed person and story, not merely name or silhouette.
- `RELATIONSHIP_RESOLVED`: characters themselves decide how to communicate/reunite.

A false lead is allowed if correction costs something or makes a relationship better/worse. Do not place a fake clue at the end of every episode. Show the *chain* from clue to consequence in the world.

**Narrative safety labels:** PROPOSED / PILOT-CANDIDATE / APPROVED-CANON / RETIRED (with reason). All new materials remain PROPOSED. Physical source approvals are separate.

## X. Mystery progress by 10 major phases
1. **EP001–100 Trust the family, not the map.** Establish safe rules; find first two verifiable route facts; outsiders (Aila/Pip etc.) have their own decisions. No named culprit yet.
2. **EP101–200 The shutdown sequence.** Compare station times, delayed failures, witness reports. Discover the network was not broken in one simultaneous instant.
3. **EP201–300 The witness Orin missed.** A sincere witness contradicts Orin's memory; the family's trust survives his willingness to verify.
4. **EP301–400 The false face of the enemy.** Someone blamed for silent bells is shown helping refugees; the family distinguishes rumors from evidence.
5. **EP401–500 Who controlled the routes?** Evan finds repeated forms of altered addresses; institutional gain becomes visible, independent of any single suspect.
6. **EP501–600 The good lock and the bad lock.** An emergency gate was closed to prevent real danger; a second unauthorized change is exposed as the cause of large-scale stranding. This is a speculative target, not pre-approved history.
7. **EP601–700 A person is not a clue.** A missing relative may be identified but has a chosen home, conflicting needs and a right to privacy; no coerced reunion.
8. **EP701–800 The second closing.** Present-day sabotage forces concrete cooperation across worlds; local allies lead their own defenses.
9. **EP801–900 A network everyone can inspect.** Distributed authority, shared testing, identity permissions and reversible safe routes are proven at community scale.
10. **EP901–1000 The truth and the table.** Responsibility is proved; family members are given the chance for honest closure; restoring the old order is rejected if it repeats harm. The final family meal pays off the pilot's dinner motif.

**Rule of scale:** a useful concrete milestone every 10–20 episodes, major reversal about every 40–60, family growth every episode, but not every milestone must reveal a villain. The 20 × 50 episode macro map in `CORE_STORY_ARCHITECTURE_1000EP_DRAFT_20261104.md` remains the provisional spine.

## XI. Example of a 45-minute causally connected family-fantasy story
**Pitch: "The Last Warm Stone" — illustrative new candidate, not a scheduled episode.**
- 00:00–02:30: A stone in Mara's workshop becomes cold while snow appears on the kitchen ceiling. A local woman appears, begging them not to ring again.
- 02:30–11:00: Her village uses warm stones to open a safe walking route. Only one path remains; the Bell family intends to carry their own lamp there.
- 11:00–21:00: Juno's lamp heats one part quickly but steals warmth from the meeting shelter. **BUT** the easy fix harms the place where sick neighbors wait.
- 21:00–32:00: Lia hears a small creature hiding under the stones; locals say the stones must rest. Mara argues with Juno about danger. **THEREFORE** they need to ask local stewards, not force more power.
- 32:00–41:30: Together, villagers rearrange their shift system; the warm path is shared and the shelter remains safe. Juno builds a simple transparent measure of stone recovery, not a monopoly on the technology.
- 41:30–45:00: Family returns to Bellfern; the woman thanks them for listening, not saving her as a passive extra. One physical object has a station mark but remains `PHYSICAL_TRACE`, not a verified family identity.
- **Character shift:** Mara acknowledges Juno's useful invention; Juno learns not every engineering output is a benefit.
- **A2 language:** warm, cold, safe, wait, share, need, first, then, together.
- **Visual engine:** reversed snow, glowing stones and warming breath generate spectacle through actor behavior, not a Ken Burns still.
- **Real run time not yet verified:** This is an act outline; a complete Fountain screenplay + timed read is required.

## XII. Editorial red-team review — what could kill the series?
1. **Lore kills wonder.** Too many names/three-key explanation early. Solution: show one visible problem and one ordinary need; save the larger conspiracy for later meaningful evidence.
2. **Mara or Orin are overpowered.** Solution: make the real obstacle a choice, an unknown variable, a social agreement or a finite destination/safety rule.
3. **Aila becomes a tool.** Solution: keep her chosen homes, own ambitions and boundaries; never define her as evidence belonging to the Bell family.
4. **Lia is always wrong, Bubu always wrong, Juno always invents.** Solution: reversal matrix; let them be competent sometimes, wrong for different human reasons, occasionally disagree without someone becoming the fool.
5. **Local people have no agency.** Solution: named resident makes a key choice in climax.
6. **Too many mistaken-family plots.** Solution: limit lookalikes/false-identity episodes; make each actual identity query costly and distinct.
7. **45 minutes becomes padding.** Solution: at least 2 material changes of plan + genuine relationship reversal + climax; test full script with timed read, no fixed word-count fiction.
8. **A2 voices sound like toddlers.** Solution: simple syntax, adult motives, purposeful silences and acting; later independent A2 comprehension test.
9. **Every episode becomes giant conspiracy.** Solution: local story is complete even when no clue is found; emotional progression is real narrative progress.
10. **The ending waits until episode 1000 to reward people.** Solution: every 50-episode book delivers at least one meaningful truth, relationship repair or technical progress.
