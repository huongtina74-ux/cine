## 1 — ONE recurring primary antagonist, TWO audience-visible personae
**Public savior persona:** a recognizable well-spoken, knowledgeable elder magic administrator / recovery leader regularly appears in rescue councils or among survivors in already accessible older worlds, delivering actual food, safe technical assistance and sincere concern. The figure has understandable moral arguments, a consistent persuasive public proposal to centralize bell routing, and an initial trust bond with Orin. Does NOT magically know Earth before its discovery.

**Masked threatening persona ("The Ash Bellkeeper" / Vietnamese "Kẻ Gác Chuông Tro"):** the SAME main antagonist adopts a practical cracked bell-mask, soot-gray cloak and audibly distorted bell-filtered voice when stealing an old address log, disabling a survivor's contact, swapping a route slate, taking rare sound-resonant material or misleading Orin about a recovered camp. The mask is an object/voice filter that can later be established from on-screen properties; it isn't a sudden new power. The identity link is hidden from audience, not a second unrelated main villain.

**Why both roles?** The audience has a visible and ACTIVE danger from the earliest episodes AND a fair-play identity puzzle. The masked figure can lose a local contest without the overarching villain disappearing; the savior can genuinely help a world yet secretly hurt another. The antagonist has a single coherent desire: take control of gateways and renewable magic to enforce an authoritarian “solution” to Veylora's real decline.

## 2 — Existing-world physical travel / strict villain geography
- In episodes before the Earth location is learned, Ash Bellkeeper travels ONLY among a few ALREADY-MAPPED WORLDS whose old fixed Gate Bells still form a connected surviving subnetwork, or remains on one such world for a stretch. He can travel with authorization/forged local credentials at gate stations, same physical mechanism as every normal traveler.
- A sabotaged destination or a rift-stranded UNKNOWN world has NO usable old station: he CANNOT simply arrive there from far away; the family might encounter ONLY old physical traps, tools, pre-disaster evidence or rumors of his agents there, not a conveniently on-scene omnipresent villain.
- On EARTH, the villain does NOT arrive before earning the location through multiple clues: a copied return-call note, a discovered rescued survivor's route, a partially recorded Bubu resonance, possibly a compromised authorized station. Such a trace is insufficient to create a new permanent gate. A costly later plot milestone involves gaining actual one-time access to or a fixed verified gate paired with Earth's anchor, and the family actively blocks it.
- Bubu's sound-echo opening does not automatically provide the villain a route; Bubu may refuse, and the Earth home return requires the actual anchor and its availability. Do not introduce "steal collar = instant access."
- The villain has finite allies, people, travel time and vulnerable tools. Showing him in two places needs real elapsed scene time or a separately established proxy, no convenient clones.
- His current scheme must serve a physical need: access to a real Gate Bell, fresh material, land-station consent, Earth's renewal data, specific Bubu memory fragment, or lost Veylora ledger. He does not scatter generic evil artifacts everywhere.

## 3 — The long GAME and immediate dramatic wants
**Before disaster:** terminal magic decline pressures society; villain believes central extraction and a monopolized gate directory is the only defensible path, but hides damage and deliberately tampered with a major resonance/route experiment. The catastrophe splits people and does not solve decline.
**After disaster, first stage:** maintain moral authority by doing selective public rescues; secretly control surviving gate notes, remove evidence of malicious changes and keep witnesses from independently comparing timelines.
**Second stage:** hear reports that some stranded witches grow STRONGER in an UNKNOWN world. Wants to identify this miracle and own it; first only has anomalies, not “Earth” as a confirmed address.
**Third stage:** recurring Ash Bellkeeper opposition to Orin and Mara to stop open decentralized rescues and gather traces of a strange living-bell portal; sometimes returns stolen things or provides genuine help to buy public trust.
**Fourth stage:** when Earth is verified, seeks its renewal conditions and Bubu's echo power, initially by bargaining/proposing a “cure,” later by coercive expedition and gate capture. He may be genuinely right that magic can heal, but wrong to force concentrated extraction.
**Fifth stage:** his true double identity and deliberate sabotage are proven through cross-checked records; villain remains active after reveal; now the story is about protecting refugees, Earth ecology and safe route governance, not stretching a single mask question for 1,000 episodes.

## 4 — 12 recurring villain-beat requirements (NOT twelve identical episodes)
1. EP001 prologue: Public figure helps Orin evacuate a village. Cut to an unidentifiable hand altering a CURRENT resonance setting; sound/shape clue only. The catastrophe's perpetrator not instantly shown.
2. EP006–010: Masked figure appears physically in a known reachable world, snatches/refiles a survivor convoy ledger. Orin pursues but protects families over the ledger. A true short action confrontation, not only shadow.
3. EP020–040: Kind public leader offers Orin supplies and limited access to a working relay; a rescue is REAL. A world helper remembers the masked figure's unusual ring rhythm—audience does not yet know who it belongs to.
4. EP050–080: A masked interference gives Mara a real practical opponent; Bubu detects an inconsistent bell echo, but can't name the person. The threatened mission is a known-world rescue, not an inexplicable villain visit to an UNKNOWN world.
5. EP081–120: First mask encounter ORIN witnesses. Masked figure leaves evidence of a rare clockwork bell-filter; public figure has a plausible reason to access similar tools, but so do others. Orin makes a reasoned false lead.
6. EP121–200: one full on-screen local villain defeat; recovered survivors keep their autonomy; side effects persist. Meanwhile public leader orchestrates a genuine rescue, complicating blame.
7. EP201–300: competing records reveal a technician created a safe diagnostic device but some later policy layer replaced safe settings. Technician cleared from initial sabotage; proof remains against unknown policy controller.
8. EP301–400: evidence of an unidentified restorative world emerges; the antagonist tries to hijack a scarred note sample but still lacks safe EARTH door and does NOT physically appear there. Family must decide whether to publish research.
9. EP401–500: grandmother or brother rescue arc contains witnessed timeline from *their own* evacuation, independently disproving a convenient story about Orin/Elara's guilt. Mask may try to destroy documentation but cannot approach an unreachable UNKNOWN world.
10. EP501–600: a near capture of the masked figure provides an unambiguous physical tool/bell component link to a specific restricted office. Don't have the villain spontaneously confess; verify through archive audit, witnesses, Bix and Evan's dated records.
11. EP601–700: antagonist double identity revealed in a concrete multi-source confrontation; motives and catastrophe responsibility distinguished from magic decline. Mask removal ALONE does not prove mass crimes; link altered settings, time and tools. Rescue teams choose saving endangered refugees over revenge.
12. EP701–1000: unmasked antagonist remains a tangible active threat pursuing exploitative Earth/Veylora “cure”; final climax uses independently learned ecological repair, safe voluntary travel and distributed governance—NOT a single Bubu super-bell button.

**Frequency guideline:** Public persona or masked antagonist appears meaningfully around every 5–10 episodes as pacing allows, not necessarily every episode, and immediate consequences can be felt via changed route rules, evidence or threatened people. Don't repeat a masked silhouette and pointless escape as filler. A single self-contained 45m local story can succeed without any villain appearance.

## 5 — Fair-play clues: plant → misread → verify → payoff
- **Signature ring pattern:** Ash Bellkeeper's pre-open check rings in a distinct rhythm. It is reproducible by different instruments; by itself NOT absolute identity proof. Its harmonic filter has a manufacturing defect first shown early.
- **Administrative routing permission:** Restricted destination edits use a narrow set of legitimate access privileges, narrowing suspects but NOT proof by authority alone.
- **Rescue ledger time anomaly:** An entry edited AFTER Orin's public evacuation order; Evan can later verify clock/address semantics after his ON-SCREEN secret discovery.
- **Material residue:** same unusual repaired relay alloy used in a public repair project and a stolen hidden tool; provenance independently checked by a technician.
- **Motives vs evidence:** a public savior has motive to control routes and direct benefit but motives are not evidence. He must be caught by independent dated originals and hardware provenance, not "Bubu felt evil."
- **Bubu's role:** hears incomplete residual note and emits a warning when a forged call claims to be Orin; cannot do fingerprint-style perfect villain detection or solve the route without Orin/Mara's research.
- **Family witnesses:** Rowan remembers who rerouted the coastal evacuation; Cael possesses partial mechanical ledger; Elara knows one-time emergency protective seal came AFTER sabotage; their independent observations converge, without omniscient memory.
- **False suspects:** rescue minister with legitimate shared tools, honest technician whose tool was repurposed, a survivor who stole food to save a camp; real alternatives are exonerated, not abandoned cliffhangers.

## 6 — Three distinct grounded sample scenes
**Scene A — Masked theft and a CHOICE:** Orin is at the mapped port station with rescued evacuees. Masked figure breaks into a locked ledger room and steals a convoy index. Orin chases him. A gate platform collapses with refugee children still on it. Orin abandons pursuit to rescue them; villain exits via a REAL, functioning mapped gate, taking the document. Ending: children saved; index lost; a ring-filter part is left as fair-play proof.
**Scene B — Public generosity with suspicious timing:** The public leader personally delivers medicine to a stricken camp, answers questions, helps an old woman find her kin. He offers Orin an "optimized" rescue register which *happens* to omit names near one malfunctioning gateway. Orin accepts the aid but Mara insists on checking the missing names independently.
**Scene C — Earth almost discovered, a strategic cliffhanger:** In a mapped world's Bell Tower, the masked figure obtains a copy of Bubu's fragmented "HOME NOTE" recorded by a damaged relay. He can hear a new sound but the destination field is blank. He orders the archive sealed and searches for a distinct resonance origin; he does NOT open an Earth portal at once. Cut to Bubu's collar bell trembling by the family's workshop garden, unaware that somebody learned part of the pattern.

## 7 — Avoid cheap plot devices / hard-fail QA
- Do not change user's new v4 premise to Earth being formerly known/forbidden; the attacker DID NOT TARGET EARTH on the disaster night.
- Do not require everyone to own small travel bells; fixed gate's bell and phrase activate known stations.
- Do not let villain freely hop uncharted worlds or arrive in Earth without previously earned access.
- Do not erase Bubu's new v4 temporary rift and bonded Earth return role; villain cannot coerce Bubu to open endless mass doors simply by stealing his collar.
- Do not present antagonist's sincere humanitarian work as fake if the survivors actually benefited; complexity is in selective care + authoritarian harm, not arbitrary mood shifts.
- Do not keep true identity secret until EP999; ~601–700 is the main identity reveal, allowing later direct open conflict.
- Do not make every lost-person case end with the villain or every episode open with the masked figure; independent world stories and local resolutions matter.
- Do not identify a new approved visual master face: villain public/ash-mask costume concept images are ART DIRECTION proposals only and require distinct approved character references, voice, disguise states and costume continuity.
- No surprise voice-changer mechanism at reveal; show it audibly and physically early.
- No unilateral main Canon overwrite, no fabricated completed cinematic/A2/full-script testing.
