## Q2. Which substantive power-user tactics and feature combinations exist in English tutorials, reviews and communities (snowballing with Get Related Videos, Browse & Extract with YouTube filters, Channels "analyze all videos", Keywords + Keywords Everywhere, Trends seasonality, Comments, Tag/Title analyzers, saved filter files)?

### Takeaway
The most concrete tactics come from the developer:
- **Snowballing**: tick rows, click **Get Related Videos**, and about 15–30 related videos per seed are added with Source = "related".
- **Freshness filter**: sort by views, then quick-filter the published date to the last 30 days.
- **Channel harvesting**: Browse & Extract with YouTube's "Channels" filter, scroll several times, then "extract channels".
- **Self-seeded discovery**: load your own (or a top) video and pull its related videos and the channels behind them.

LearnWire adds:
- a **channel scrape → CSV → ChatGPT** pipeline that turned a channel's titles into 50 topic ideas;
- reading **Trends "breakout"** related queries over 30 days, 90 days or 12 months.

No source shows the full stack (Browse & Extract with upload-date or Shorts filters → custom filter file → per-channel consistency check) as one recorded workflow. That combination has to be assembled from documented pieces.

### Inferences
These "power recipes" are assembled only from documented features. Each is INFERRED; none was recorded end to end by a source.
1. **Booming-now scan (long-form or Shorts):**
   1. Browse & Extract: search the seed on YouTube inside the app.
   2. Apply YouTube's **Upload date = Today or This week** filter (and **Type = Shorts**, or a duration filter, when relevant). Do not use the old "sort by upload date", which YouTube removed per Apify.
   3. Auto-scroll and extract.
   4. In the grid, quick-filter **Publish Age in Days ≤ 7** and sort by **Average Views per Day**.
   5. Add a custom filter **Total Channel Subscribers ≤ N** (OWNER decision) to surface small channels with spikes.
   6. Save it as a filter file so the scan can be repeated every night.
2. **Snowball:**
   1. Tick the top 5–10 rows from recipe 1 and click **Get Related Videos**. Expect about 15–30 per seed, per the developer demo.
   2. Filter Source = "related".
   3. Re-sort by views/day.
   4. Repeat one more hop.

   This expands from keyword space into YouTube's recommendation graph. That graph is where faceless "suggested-traffic" niches live.
3. **Long-lasting check ("consistent winner"):**
   1. Send candidate channels to Channels → **analyze all videos**.
   2. Sort by views/day.
   3. Look at whether many videos older than about 180 days still earn views/day. This is the evergreen pattern in the Vidsilo example.
   4. Export to CSV and compute median views per video and an outlier multiple (views ÷ channel median) in `tools/cf_research_stats.py`, because Tube Atlas has no outlier column.
4. **Seasonality and demand:** Keywords (autosuggest) → Trends over 12 months on the YouTube source, read the averages and trend slopes → Comments on the top 3–5 videos, filtering questions and word combinations, for pain points → Title and Tag analyzers on the winners' grid for packaging patterns.
5. **Self-seeded graph (developer tactic 4):** once the owner has one published video in a niche, put it into Browse/Import and pull its related videos. This reveals which channels YouTube already groups it with.

## Q4. What common mistakes and limits do users report (search sources pulling old videos, first-page-only searches, rate limits, flat earnings estimate)?

### Takeaway
Documented limits:
1. **Plain search is shallow.** Demos search "the first page" or "first five pages". The vendor's own beta tester says search gave "only 1 page of results" until he used Browse & Extract.
2. **Rate limiting.** YouTube's "too many requests" limit forced extraction changes in v1.0.6.6 (Apr 2026). Google Trends is restricted for fast extraction.
3. **Earnings columns are rough estimates.** Reviewers say "not 100% perfect"; Social Blade-style flat RPM logic does not reflect niche, country or Shorts.
4. **Long-tail queries return little** in unlisted search (use 1–2 words).
5. **YouTube removed "sort by upload date"** from search, so recency must come from the upload-date filter plus in-grid sorting.

I found no source for the claim that the Google/Bing search sources "pull old videos". That remains unverified.

### Inferences
Common mistakes to avoid (INFERRED from the limits above):
1. **Judging a niche from one keyword search page.** Use Browse & Extract with scroll, Get Related Videos, and several short keywords to get past the about 500–600 cap.
2. **Ranking niches by "Estimated Earnings".** It is views × an assumed rate. Use views/day, upload consistency and the niche median instead, and treat RPM as "not measured".
3. **Treating the top video as the benchmark.** CineFactory research policy uses the niche MEDIAN.
4. **Hammering extraction** (large batches, rapid re-runs). This trips YouTube/Google "too many requests". Batch small, wait between runs, and keep the app updated. The vendor removed proxy support from Trends, so proxies are not a supported fix.
5. **Using the spinner, downloader, Creative Commons "legal content" or Video-to-Text output as content.** This breaches CineFactory's original-IP rule and risks YouTube reused-content enforcement. Use transcripts only as research notes.
6. **Recency confusion.** Since YouTube removed sort-by-date, "newest" results must come from the upload-date filter in the built-in browser plus the grid's Publish Age column.
