## 1. Which channels win with 3D recreations/reconstructions — start dates, subs, views/video, length, cadence, team

### Takeaway
There are two very different winning shapes: (a) **news-reactive 3D crash/disaster reconstructions** (Rewynd3D, Fault Lines, TALEYE, reci, Reconstructed, AP Way) where brand-new channels under 10K subs got 0.5–4.4M views on one breaking event in Nov 2026 but collapse to 10K–60K views on the next non-news topic; and (b) **evergreen "how it worked / what really happened" 3D engineering-history channels** (Animagraffs, Deconstructed, Oceanliner Designs, Mustard) that average ~1–4M views per video but upload slowly (every 3–12 weeks) with teams or years of tooling. Map-animation military history (Kings and Generals, The Operations Room, Historia Civilis) is large but mostly 2D maps, outsourced/team-built, and Kings and Generals itself reports WW2 videos cost more and get half the views.

### Inferences
- INFERRED: For news-reactive crash channels, per-video views vary ~40–400x between a US breaking crash and a non-breaking topic (Rewynd3D 2.4M vs 58K; TALEYE 4.45M vs 9.4K). Income is lumpy and depends on being first within ~24–96 h after a crash.
- INFERRED: Competition on one event is intense: at least 6 3D channels and 6+ pilot channels each got ≥0.5M views on one Learjet crash. The format is crowded, and its edge comes from speed, not 3D quality.
- INFERRED: The evergreen 3D "how it worked / what really happened" format (Deconstructed, Animagraffs, Oceanliner) gives the best views per video and builds a back catalogue. Deconstructed grew from zero (Aug 2023) to 439K subs with 50 videos, about 1.1M avg views/video (55.4M/50), using a ~9-person credited team.
- INFERRED: Rough benchmark cadence: solo with custom tooling ≈ 1 video / 2–3 months (Animagraffs); small team ≈ 1 video / 2–4 weeks (Deconstructed, Operations Room); larger team/distributor ≈ 2/week (Oceanliner).

## 2. How these are made, time per video, and what can be automated (Blender/Python/procedural/AI)

### Takeaway
The evergreen leaders build models in Blender (Animagraffs, Branch Education, Jared Owen) or SketchUp (Mustard). They buy or license stock and royalty-free models (Mustard, Deconstructed) or license whole reconstructions (Lost in Time with Rome Reborn), and finish in After Effects or Premiere. Hero-model building is the bottleneck: Animagraffs says a model alone can take a month or more. Python helps most where the work repeats: scene assembly from data (ADS-B flight paths, OSM buildings, DEM terrain), camera rigs, labels/infographics, and render batching. Animagraffs itself moved to self-coded Blender tools.

### Cited Findings

That can take a month or more in itself"; uses Blender for modelling/texturing/effects and Adobe software for layout and export (2019 interview) — OBSERVED [Technical Illustrators](https://technicalillustrators.org/2019/08/animagraffs-by-jacob-oneal). ### Inferences
- INFERRED: Automatable with Blender Python / procedural tools:
  - flight or ship track import (CSV/KML → curve → object + camera follow)
  - terrain from public DEMs
  - city/environment blocks from OSM via Blosm and geometry nodes
  - weather volumes and particle "storm" presets
  - text/label callouts and timelines driven from a JSON script
  - camera move templates (orbit, follow, cutaway, X-ray/section)
  - batch rendering
  - edit/VO/music assembly (CineFactory already has `cf_auto_edit.py`)
- INFERRED: Hard to automate, and the place where quality is won: accurate hero models (aircraft/ship/structure) and the research that makes them correct. A Learjet 45 model used for a Learjet 60 shows the speed-over-accuracy shortcut that aviation viewers notice.
- INFERRED: AI is a fit for script drafting and fact-gathering support, TTS narration and thumbnails (TALEYE discloses AI thumbnails). The "No AI" labels on established channels suggest AI-generated *visuals* are becoming a negative signal for this audience. Keep the 3D original and procedural, and use AI for text and voice.
- INFERRED effort estimate (ESTIMATED from the above, not measured in CineFactory): one 20–30 min evergreen 3D documentary is about 2–4 person-weeks once a reusable asset library exists. News-reactive 10–20 min reconstructions in 1–4 days need a pre-built kit (aircraft families, airport/terrain/weather, cockpit set) ready before events happen.

### Gaps
- No creator gave hours-per-video figures beyond Animagraffs' "a month or more" for the model; Fault Lines, TALEYE and Rewynd3D methods (Blender vs Unreal vs AI-image-to-3D) are not disclosed.
- Not measured: CineFactory's own render time per minute for this style on the RTX 3060 12 GB.

## 3. Monetization: RPM category, sensitive-event ad limits, YouTube policy on recreations of real tragedies, news-cycle vs evergreen

### Takeaway
Evergreen history/engineering 3D content sits in the best-paying measured category (Education & Science, median RPM $10.22; Transport $5.69). Breaking-news crash coverage falls under YouTube's "Sensitive events" and violence/shocking rules. Documentary context can keep ads on, but it is the riskiest part of the format for limited ads, and its views do not compound. A major new constraint: from **1 Feb 2027 new channels need 8,000 watch hours (not 4,000)** plus 1K subs to join YPP.

### Cited Findings
- AIR Media-Tech (YouTube Studio data, 300 channels, 3,595 monetized channel-months, May 2025–May 2026): median RPM Education & Science **$10.22** (P25–P75 $2.31–$19.50), Transport* **$5.69** ($3.17–$9.81), News & Politics* **$2.60** ($1.79–$3.62), Entertainment $2.43, all-niche median $2.30 (*fewer than ten channels) — OBSERVED [ClipFlux summary 2026-10-10](https://clipflux.ai/guides/youtube-rpm-by-niche), [AIR glossary 2026-08-25](https://air.io/en/youtube-glossary/what-is-youtube-rpm-full-breakdown)
- In that data, about 77% of Education views served an ad, the best rate of any niche; Transport ranks second mainly on ad load, "2.55 ads per monetized view on long car, aviation and build videos" — OBSERVED [ClipFlux](https://clipflux.ai/guides/youtube-rpm-by-niche). A median channel monetizes only 53% of its views — OBSERVED [AIR](https://air.io/en/youtube-glossary/what-is-youtube-rpm-full-breakdown)
- Advertiser-friendly guidelines, Violence: "Content where the focal point is on blood, violence, or injury, when presented without other context, is not suitable … If you're showing violent content in a news, educational, artistic, or documentary context, that additional context is important" — OBSERVED [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)
- Same page lists as an example "Coverage of tragedies involving one or more deaths (excluding sensitive events such as mass shootings or terrorist attacks) with limited or no display of violent acts or their results", and "Fully obscured (such as blurred out), graphic dead bodies" — OBSERVED [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en&visit_id=639203298475509138-2104441959&rd=1) (the ad tier, green vs yellow, for this line was not visible in the fetched excerpt)
- Sensitive events: "Examples … include civil emergencies, natural disasters, public health emergencies, terrorism and related activities, conflict, or mass acts of violence. This policy applies even if the content contains no graphic imagery." Ukraine-war content that "exploits, dismisses, or condones the war is ineligible for monetization" (since 2022-03-23) — OBSERVED [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)
- Shocking content: "Content that may upset, disgust, or shock viewers may not be suitable … context matters"; Controversial issues covers suicide, self-harm, abuse, etc. — OBSERVED [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)
- YouTube reviews title, thumbnail, description and tags too; adding educational/documentary context can change suitability; human review can be requested — OBSERVED [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9348366?hl=en)
- Google Ads side: sensitive-event rules prohibit "Claims that victims of a sensitive event were responsible for their own tragedy or similar instances of victim blaming" — OBSERVED [Google Ads](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16489952?hl=en)

- "Inauthentic content" (renamed from repetitious, 2025-07-15) covers mass-produced/repetitive content, e.g. "slideshows that all have the same narration"; YouTube says channels using AI remain eligible — OBSERVED [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en), [TeamYouTube 2025-07-10](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/356734251?hl=en&msgid=408058933)

AIR Media-Tech states applications reaching YouTube by 23:59 UTC Jan 31 2027 are judged against the old 4,000-hour bar (per their partner support, not in YouTube docs) — OBSERVED [AIR](https://air.io/en/youtube-glossary/what-is-youtube-rpm-full-breakdown)
- Ethics practice seen on winning channels: memorial lines naming victims, "3D scenes are reconstructions" notes, source lists (Rewynd3D, reci, Reconstructed, TALEYE); Mentour's podcast: "we know way too little to speculate" — OBSERVED [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lNA03U4litA), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NHOOknJyAaE), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f7cknYUrkH8). ### Inferences
- INFERRED: Evergreen engineering/historical 3D documentaries (pre-1950 disasters, "how it worked", ships/aircraft/structures) should earn in the Education ($10.22) or Transport ($5.69) bands. Recent-crash videos are more likely to be classed News ($2.60) and face sensitive-event or limited-ads friction, so the same views may earn about 2–4x less.
- INFERRED: Example at the measured medians: 1M views × $10.22 RPM ≈ $10.2K; at $2.60 ≈ $2.6K. These are not channel-specific measurements.
- INFERRED: The 8,000-hour YPP bar changes the maths for a new channel. At an assumed 8-min average view duration, it needs about 60,000 views (8,000 × 60 / 8). One viral video can cover it, but a slow evergreen start may take months. A channel started now cannot realistically reach even the old 4,000-hour bar before 2027-01-31.
- INFERRED ethics rules for the studio:
  - no reconstructions of events under about 30 days old, and no ongoing sensitive events (mass shootings, terrorism, active wars)
  - no victim blaming or "should X face charges" engagement bait
  - clear on-screen "reconstruction, not footage" notes, a source list, and no recreated deaths or bodies
  - for the 9/11-type topics Rewynd3D covers, expect limited ads and reputational risk

### Gaps
- No measured RPM specifically for 3D disaster/aviation-crash channels; whether YouTube classifies them as News, Education or Transport for advertisers is not public.
- The exact ad tier (green/yellow) for "coverage of tragedies involving deaths" and for animated depictions of fatal accidents was not visible in the fetched guideline text. The policy's internal "sensitive event" designations (which events and how long) are not published.
- No reliable source found on viewer or family backlash against fast 3D crash reconstructions (r/aviation search returned nothing usable).

## 4. Asset licensing for monetized videos

### Takeaway
The safe stack is Poly Haven (CC0), BlenderKit/Blendkit (royalty-free or CC0, commercial use OK, no attribution needed, no resale), USGS/public-domain terrain, and OSM data with an "OpenStreetMap" credit. Sketchfab free models are usable only if the licence is CC BY (credit + link) or CC0, never NC. Google Earth Studio is allowed in monetized educational/entertainment YouTube videos only with the on-screen attribution kept for the whole time the imagery is visible.

### Cited Findings
- Poly Haven: all HDRIs, textures and models are CC0; commercial use allowed; no credit required — OBSERVED [Poly Haven License](https://polyhaven.com/license), [FAQ](https://docs.polyhaven.com/en/faq)

### Inferences
- INFERRED: A monetized YouTube channel is commercial use. Sketchfab CC BY-NC and Editorial-licensed models should be excluded. Keep a per-asset licence ledger (source URL, licence, credit line) like CineFactory's verification receipts.
- INFERRED: Real aircraft and ship models often carry airline liveries and trademarks. Prefer generic liveries for recent crashes, or build original models from public drawings.
- INFERRED: The Google photorealistic 3D Tiles route via Blosm adds a billing account, EU restrictions and Maps Platform terms. For an original-IP Blender pipeline, OSM + public DEM (USGS/Copernicus) + own models is cleaner.

### Gaps
- Not verified: Google Maps Platform terms for rendering Photorealistic 3D Tiles into a monetized video; Microsoft Flight Simulator / X-Plane content-usage terms; Flightradar24 and LiveATC terms for commercial reuse of ADS-B data and ATC audio (several winners use both); Copernicus DEM licence wording.

## 5. Gaps and opportunities: under-served topics and demand signals

### Takeaway
Demand is proven for pre-1950 and engineering-failure topics told as "what really happened / how it worked" in 3D. Hindenburg (3.15M), Britannic (5.17M), Lusitania (4.5M), Golden Gate/Hoover Dam (3.6M/4.4M) and ancient Rome (1.57M) are evergreen. Breaking crashes give spikes, but the space is crowded with 10+ channels per event. Mining/industrial and non-US disasters show weak pull. The open lane for a Blender + Python studio is evergreen historical disasters and engineering builds with accurate original 3D, produced faster than the 1-video-per-quarter craft channels.

### Cited Findings
- Evergreen pre-1950 disaster/engineering demand: Deconstructed Hindenburg 3.15M (Jun 2026); Oceanliner Britannic 5.17M, Lusitania real time 4.5M; Animagraffs Hoover Dam 4.4M, Golden Gate 3.59M, 16th-century sailing ship 7.1M; Titanic Honor & Glory real-time sinking 97M — OBSERVED [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@deconstructed_animations/videos/rQl3GRPxAEI), [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@oceanlinerdesigns), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@animagraffs/videos), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/titanichg)

### Inferences
- INFERRED best-fit topic list (evergreen, Education/Transport RPM, low sensitive-event risk, procedural-friendly):
  - historical disasters before ~1950: Hindenburg-type airships, Tay Bridge, Quebec Bridge, Johnstown flood, Halifax explosion, St. Francis Dam, Boston molasses flood, SS Eastland, Sultana
  - shipwrecks and liners: compete with Oceanliner by niche-down, e.g. lesser-known wrecks, real-time sinkings
  - "how it was built": dams, bridges, canals, cathedrals, Roman aqueducts
  - ancient engineering cities: Pompeii, Rome districts, Constantinople walls
  - scale/space 3D as a secondary series
- INFERRED: Avoid making recent crashes the main channel. If used, treat them as a separate, opt-in "explainer after official preliminary report" series (e.g. 30+ days after the event) to limit sensitive-event, ethics and accuracy risk.
- INFERRED: Mining and industrial accidents are under-served but showed low pull in the samples here. Test 1–2 as part of a 6-video gate rather than as the core.
- INFERRED: Thumbnail/title packaging matters. Deconstructed's Hindenburg retitle from "How the Hindenburg Actually Worked (and Why It Failed)" to "We Finally Know What Actually Happened To The Hindenburg" is the pattern ("what really happened") the winners use.
