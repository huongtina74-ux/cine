# Minecraft YouTube sub-niches: long-term sustainability and platform/IP risks (2025–2026)

Research date: 2026-11-04. Public web pages only, no YouTube API, no downloads. Labels used: **[OFFICIAL]** = first-party (Microsoft/Mojang/YouTube/FTC/court/WIPO); **[PRESS]** = news outlet reporting; **[3P-DATA]** = third-party analytics estimates (vidIQ, Stream Hatchet, MilX/AIR, trendreport); **[ANECDOTE]** = creator/Reddit/self-description. ## 1. Minecraft audience health (players, movie, update cadence, share of YouTube viewing)

### Takeaway
Minecraft's audience is at or near its all-time high. Microsoft reported a record 155M monthly active users for Jul–Sep 2025, a record quarter for usage and revenue in Apr–Jun 2025 that it credited "in large part" to the movie, and per a secondary report a new record for players and revenue again in Jul–Sep 2026. The film grossed about $958–961M and a sequel is dated for 23 July 2027. Mojang now ships 3–4 free "game drops" a year plus two Minecraft LIVE shows, and has announced the first new dimension in 14 years for 2027. Minecraft is still one of YouTube's biggest games, but in 2025 live-stream hours Roblox overtook it.

### Inferences
- The underlying IP is not the risk. MAU, the film, the 2027 sequel, the theme park, Dungeons II, a new dimension in 2027 and a steady drop cadence all point to multi-year audience support through at least 2027. Every drop and LIVE show is a recurring, predictable topic window for update, explainer and building content.
- The movie mostly reactivated lapsed and younger players (54% lapsed, per Ampere). Kids-skewed demand is high, which pulls channels toward Made-for-Kids economics (see Q2).
- Roblox is a real competitor for the same young audience, and on live hours it is now ahead of Minecraft on YouTube.

### Gaps
- YouTube has not published a cumulative Minecraft view total since 1.5T (May 2024). I found no official 2025 or 2026 figure for Minecraft's share of YouTube gaming VOD views.

## 2. YouTube policy risks (inauthentic content, AI disclosure, Made for Kids/COPPA, reused content)

### Takeaway
The main monetization risk is YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule, renamed from "repetitious content" on 15 July 2025 and clarified in July 2026 into three non-monetizable categories. It targets mass-produced, template, or generic channels regardless of whether AI was used. Enforcement on top AI-slop channels has been real since November 2025, including deletions of channels with billions of views. AI disclosure is mandatory only for *realistic* synthetic content, so cartoons and blocky animation are exempt, but auto-labeling began in May 2026. For kids-skewed Minecraft content, Made for Kids/COPPA is the largest structural revenue issue (third-party median kids RPM of about $0.33 versus about $2.05 for gaming), and the FTC fined Disney $10M in 2025 for mislabeling.

### Cited Findings
**Inauthentic content (July 2025) and 2026 clarification**
- [OFFICIAL] On 15 July 2025, YouTube made a "minor update" to the "repetitious content" policy "to better clarify this includes content that is repetitive or mass-produced," and renamed it "inauthentic content." The reused content policy was unchanged. Monetized content must "Not be mass-produced, generic, repetitive, or manipulative." — [YouTube Help, channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- [OFFICIAL] TeamYouTube FAQ (10 Jul 2025): the guidelines "apply regardless of how the content was made," and "channels that use AI in their content remain eligible to monetize." Examples of mass-produced content: "A channel that uploads narrated stories with only superficial differences between them" and "slideshows that all have the same narration." — [YouTube Community post](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/356734251?hl=en&msgid=357092149)
- [PRESS] July 2026 clarification, described by Tubefilter as a communications fix and not a new rule. Three non-monetizable categories: (1) generic or repetitive content, (2) "unsatisfying or off-putting" content, such as repeated disturbing themes without a narrative, templates of animals in distress, or stitched unrelated AI clips with "no clear narrative arc," and (3) fake AI "experts." — [Tubefilter, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update); [Engadget, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.engadget.com/2226445/youtube-explains-ai-slop-policy-why-some-creators-wont-get-paid/)
- [OFFICIAL] Neal Mohan's 2026 letter (21 Jan 2026) has a section titled "Managing AI slop" and builds on existing anti-spam, clickbait and low-quality-repetitive systems. More than 1M channels used YouTube's AI creation tools daily in Dec 2025. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/the-future-of-youtube-2026)
- [OFFICIAL, investor transcript] Mohan (14 May 2026) explicitly cited Minecraft culture as the kind of "emerging forms of creativity that might have seemed strange at first" that YouTube does not want to suppress, while saying "AI slop is not something … you're going to see prevalence of." — [MoffettNathanson transcript](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_events/2026/May/14/Neal-Mohan-MoffettNathanson-Transcript-05-14-26.pdf)

**Enforcement evidence**
- [PRESS] Kapwing (Nov 2025) estimated about 21% of videos shown to new users are "AI slop." It found 278 all-slop channels with 63bn views, 221M subscribers and about $117M a year in revenue. — [The Guardian, 27 Dec 2025](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2025/dec/27/more-than-20-of-videos-shown-to-new-youtube-users-are-ai-slop-study-finds)
- [PRESS] YouTube removed top AI-slop channels, including CuentosFacianantes (5.9M subscribers, 1.2B views, Dragon Ball-themed). Kapwing counted 16 of the top 100 AI channels deleted from Nov 2025 to Jan 2026. — [The Verge, 28 Jan 2026](https://www.theverge.com/news/869684/youtube-top-ai-channels-removed-kapwing); [Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/world/us-streamers/youtube-deletes-16-top-ai-generated-channels-report-finds/articleshow/127828018.cms)
- [PRESS] After a New York Times investigation of AI kids' videos, YouTube suspended 5 channels from YPP and removed videos under child-safety rules (Feb 2026). Dexerto reports false positives: an "AI slop detector incorrectly targets Kurzgesagt," and animators were demonetized for using dinosaurs. — [Dexerto, 27 Feb 2026](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-strikes-down-channels-after-investigation-into-bizarre-ai-content-for-kids-3326231)
- [PRESS] WIRED (May 2025) found AI "cartoon gore" channels aimed at kids, and YouTube terminated or demonetized several, raising "Elsagate" comparisons. **(OLDER DATA)** The Elsagate cleanup removed ads from 2M+ videos and terminated 270 accounts. — [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/dozens-of-youtube-channels-are-showing-ai-generated-cartoon-gore-and-fetish-content); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/05/02/ai-elsagate-videos-minion-go-cat-child-safety-content/)
- [PRESS] More than 200 groups (Fairplay letter, 1 Apr 2026) asked Google to ban AI-generated content from YouTube Kids and label all AI content. YouTube said it limits AI content in YouTube Kids "to a small set of high-quality channels." — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/01/ai-slop-200-organizations-letter-youtube-google/); [NBC/AP](https://www.nbcchicago.com/news/national-international/advocacy-groups-urge-youtube-protect-kids-ai-slop-videos/3916830)
- [PRESS] After LA School Report/The 74 reporting (updated 27 Mar 2026), YouTube terminated 6 channels for terms violations and 1 for spam. — [LA School Report](https://www.laschoolreport.com/ai-slop-is-flooding-childrens-media-parents-should-be-very-alarmed)
- [ANECDOTE] A creator told Digiday his channel was *terminated*, not just demonetized, in a repetitive-content crackdown: "The number of channels being terminated is kind of insane." Marketers welcomed the change. — [Digiday, 18 Jul 2025](https://digiday.com/media/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-has-creators-concerned-marketers-cheering)
- No Minecraft-specific case found: no reputable report named a Minecraft channel demonetized specifically under "inauthentic content." The examples above are kids/AI-cartoon channels in general.

**AI disclosure**
- [OFFICIAL / PRESS] Disclosure is required only for *realistic* altered or synthetic content. Clearly unrealistic or animated content is exempt. — [NBC/AP](https://www.nbcchicago.com/news/national-international/advocacy-groups-urge-youtube-protect-kids-ai-slop-videos/3916830); [YouTube Blog 2026 letter](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/the-future-of-youtube-2026)
- [PRESS] Since 27 May 2026, YouTube auto-applies an AI label when its systems detect "significant photorealistic AI use." Labels are more prominent (below the player on long-form, a persistent overlay on Shorts). — [Thurrott](https://www.thurrott.com/a-i/336552/youtube-now-automatically-applies-more-visible-labels-on-ai-generated-videos)

**Made for Kids / COPPA / FTC**
- [OFFICIAL] Creators must set MFK status under the FTC agreement. "Failure to set your content appropriately may result in consequences on YouTube or have legal consequences under COPPA." Content can count as MFK even when kids are not the primary audience, if characters, activities or games reflect intent to target children. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076?hl=en)
- [OFFICIAL] FTC/DOJ v. Disney: Disney paid a $10M civil penalty, announced 2 Sep 2025 and approved by the court in Dec 2025, for mislabeling kid-directed YouTube videos as "Not Made for Kids" through channel-level defaults. It must run a per-video audience-review program "unless YouTube implements age assurance technologies." — [FTC press release, Dec 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/court-approves-order-requiring-disney-pay-10-million-settle-ftc-allegations-firm-enabled-unlawful); [FTC Sep 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/disney-pay-10-million-settle-ftc-allegations-company-enabled-unlawful-collection-childrens-personal)
- [OFFICIAL] The amended COPPA Rule took effect 23 June 2025, with a compliance date of 22 April 2026. It adds separate parental consent for targeted ads and data-retention limits. — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/html/2025/04/22/2025-05904.html)
- [3P-DATA] The per-violation COPPA civil penalty is $53,088 (2025 inflation adjustment). — [Gyre](https://gyre.pro/blog/how-to-monetize-a-youtube-kids-channel)
- [3P-DATA] MilX/AIR Media-Tech panel of 300 monetized channels (May 2025–May 2026): median RPM for Kids & Teens $0.33 (middle 50% $0.16–0.88; median CPM $1.59; ads on only 32% of kids views) versus Gaming $2.05 (middle 50% $0.70–3.62) and an all-niche median of $2.30. — [MilX RPM guide](https://milx.app/en/guidelines/cpm-vs-rpm-the-key-metrics-for-youtube-monetization); [MilX kids guide](https://milx.app/en/guidelines/guide-to-monetize-videos-in-youtube-kids-niche)
- [3P-DATA, unconfirmed by YouTube] Industry sources put the MFK RPM drop at 50–90%+. — [Makeinfluence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/youtubes-made-for-kids-coppa-classification-what-it-does-to-ad-revenue)

**Reused content**
- [OFFICIAL] Unchanged in 2025. Commentary, clips, compilations and reactions can monetize with "significant original commentary, modifications, or educational or entertainment value." — [YouTube Community post](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/356734251?hl=en&msgid=357092149)

### Inferences
- The most exposed Minecraft sub-niches are high-volume template formats: AI-voiced "story" channels, near-identical Shorts loops, AI-clip compilations, and fan-character channels uploading daily with interchangeable plots. These match YouTube's own examples ("narrated stories with only superficial differences"). Termination, not just demonetization, is a documented outcome.
- Original animation with a real narrative arc, consistent characters and visible craft is explicitly protected. YouTube's CEO cited Minecraft culture as creativity to protect.
- Kids-skewed Minecraft animation faces a two-sided trap. Labeling MFK cuts RPM by roughly 6x versus gaming (third-party medians). Not labeling it when the content is child-directed creates COPPA liability (Disney precedent, $53K per violation). A durable business needs either a genuinely general/teen audience or revenue beyond AdSense (licensing, merch, live streams; Maizen's pocket.watch deal is the model, see Q4).
- Auto AI-labeling currently targets photorealism, so the "realistic AI Steve/Alex" style is the most label-exposed Minecraft format.

### Gaps
- I found no named, verifiable Minecraft channel demonetized under the July 2025 inauthentic-content rule, and no Reddit thread confirmed one within the time box.
- YouTube does not publish how many channels were demonetized under inauthentic content.

## 3. IP/legal risks (Mojang guidelines, fan animation of other creators' characters, Sprunki/Poppy Playtime)

### Takeaway
Mojang permits ad-monetized Minecraft videos if they are free to view and contain "enough of your own unique content." Mojang can revoke permission at its discretion. "Minecraft" may not be the primary or dominant word in a channel or series name, and nothing may imply official status, with a disclaimer recommended. Maizen's own Fan Content Guidelines allow ad-monetized derivative videos with its characters, which is why the large wave of JJ & Mikey fan channels exists. That permission is revocable and excludes impersonation, re-uploads and brand-harming content. Since Oct 2025 a China licensee also claims exclusive rights and enforces them. Sprunki and Poppy Playtime show that viral kids IPs are actively enforced (WIPO domain cases, mod takedowns, a Mob Entertainment lawsuit against Google).

### Cited Findings
**Mojang/Microsoft Usage Guidelines**
- [OFFICIAL] You may make money from videos and streams "if": all videos are free to view, and "You add enough of your own unique content … for example: You can add your own audio commentary … You can't just include your logo, web address, or overlays on top of our or someone else's gameplay – we reserve our right to decide on if your content is unique enough." Sponsors and callouts must sit outside game content and be suitable for children. No selling copies; ads around YouTube videos are allowed ("we're cool with this"). — [minecraft.net Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)
- [OFFICIAL] Naming: "You may not use the Minecraft name as the primary or dominant name or title." It may appear in a secondary name, title or description only when "necessary to describe your creations … honestly." No Minecraft logo or brand assets in your branding. — [minecraft.net Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)
- [OFFICIAL] Don't imply official or endorsed status. "Prominently include the disclaimer similar to … 'NOT AN OFFICIAL MINECRAFT [PRODUCT/SERVICE/EVENT/etc.]. NOT APPROVED BY OR ASSOCIATED WITH MOJANG OR MICROSOFT'." Content must not harm the brand (gambling, pornography, violence and other mature content). "All permissions … may be revoked at any time if … we don't like what you are doing." — [minecraft.net Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)
- [OFFICIAL] Using Minecraft to promote unrelated brands, movies or TV is not allowed without written approval, including "Minecraft blocks used to build a world or characters from a movie." — [minecraft.net Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)

**Maizen / JJ & Mikey fan animation**

Conditions: add "a sufficient amount of your creative works," no simple reproduction (clips and shorts allowed), no implied official status, nothing harming brand value, nothing cruel or abusive. "We may withdraw the permission … at any time … at our discretion." — [maizen.com usage guidelines](https://www.maizen.com/usage-guidelines)

Temma Animation (488K subscribers) uses the same disclaimer (3.1M-view video, Aug 2026). - [PRESS] Skibidi Toilet IP turbulence: a disputed DMCA claim was filed against Garry's Mod over Skibidi characters (Jul 2024), and there was creator/licensor controversy in Oct–Nov 2025. — [Wikipedia: Skibidi Toilet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skibidi_Toilet)

### Inferences
- Building a channel on another creator's characters (JJ & Mikey) is legal today *because the owner chose to allow it*. That permission is unilateral and revocable, and Maizen's incentives are shifting now that it has a TV/toy/book franchise, a pocket.watch partner and a China licensee enforcing exclusivity. A franchise owner has growing reasons to tighten fan permissions, though none has been announced. A fan channel has no equity in the characters, cannot license them, and its library becomes worthless if permission is withdrawn.
- Trend-IP characters (Sprunki, Poppy Playtime, Skibidi) are commercially owned and actively policed, and include registered copyrights. Using them carries real strike and takedown risk, beyond the Minecraft guidelines.
- The safe structure is: original characters and world, "Minecraft" only as a secondary descriptor, a Mojang disclaimer, no Mojang logo in branding, and enough original content (story, voice, animation) to pass Mojang's "unique content" test. This matches the CineFactory hard rule "original IP only."

### Gaps
- I found no public record of Maizen or pocket.watch issuing strikes against fan animators on YouTube, and no announced change to the 2021 guidelines.
- I did not verify whether the Maizen guidelines formally cover "JJ" by that name (they list ZENICHI). Fan channels assume coverage.
- I could not confirm a YouTube-level mass takedown of Sprunki videos, only domain/mod enforcement.

## 4. Trend lifecycle evidence (booms versus steady formats)

### Takeaway
Viral sub-formats burn out fast at the *trend-channel* level but persist as *title devices* on established brands. Skibidi Toilet's channel fell from billions of monthly views in mid-2023 to about 374M in May 2024. "100 Days" peaked around 2019–2021 and survives as a recurring episode format on big channels. Maizen-derivative fan channels keep appearing (some gain about 700K subscribers in 5 months) but individual copycats decay to almost nothing within about a year of their last upload. The durable assets are owned characters and long-running series (Maizen itself, Hermitcraft, LDShadowLady), which keep pulling views across years.

### Inferences
- Durability ranking (INFERRED from the evidence above):
  1. **Durable, multi-year:** owned original characters or ensemble series with continuing story (Maizen as owner, Aphmau, Hermitcraft, Life series). Building, survival and educational content tied to the drop cadence. Update and explainer content timed to two LIVE shows and 3–4 drops a year.
  2. **Medium:** challenge-format devices ("100 Days," "Minecraft But…", NOOB vs PRO) used as titles by an established brand. The format persists even though the standalone trend faded.
  3. **High risk / fad:** channels whose identity is a third party's characters (JJ & Mikey copies) or a viral meme IP (Skibidi, Sprunki, Poppy). Individual channels peak in months and decay within about 12 months of stopping. They depend on revocable permission and on YouTube's tolerance for template output.
  4. **Highest risk:** mass-produced AI-generated Minecraft (AI-voiced story loops, "realistic Steve/Alex" AI clips). These match inauthentic-content examples, draw auto AI labels when photorealistic, and come under kids-content scrutiny.
- Skibidi's curve (about 16B views in 16 months, then roughly a 90% drop) is the best documented timeline for a Gen-Alpha meme wave: roughly 12–18 months.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative public data on the "100 Days" genre's view decline after 2021, and YouTube has not published updated genre data.
- I found no reputable press coverage of a specific "AI realistic Steve & Alex" Minecraft wave (searches returned only generic Veo tutorials). Its existence and size are unverified.
- Copycat-channel trajectories come from vidIQ public snapshots, not a systematic sample.

## 5. Brand safety and advertiser sentiment (kids animation and gaming)

### Takeaway
Advertisers welcomed YouTube's anti-slop moves. Kids inventory is structurally low-value on YouTube (no personalized ads, about 32% ad fill, about a $1.59 median CPM per one panel), video-game ads are banned on YouTube Kids, and kids-marketing watchdogs are active. Kids attention on YouTube is still very large, so brand money flows to safe, story-driven, family-trusted IP (licensing and brand deals) rather than to AdSense on kids views.

### Cited Findings
- [PRESS] "Marketers like YouTube's revised AI policies, but creators are wary" (Digiday, Jul 2025). — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-has-creators-concerned-marketers-cheering)
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube Kids ad policy: no interest-based ads, no remarketing pixels, every paid ad pre-approved, and **Video Games** among the prohibited ad categories. YouTube Kids also excludes videos with branded content or endorsements and "overly commercial" content. — [YouTube Help: Advertising on YouTube Kids](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6168681?hl=en); [YouTube Kids content policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10938174?hl=en)
- [OFFICIAL] Advertiser-friendly guidelines: "Violence in unedited video gameplay is generally acceptable for advertising, but montages where gratuitous violence is the focal point is not." MFK content must follow the quality principles for kids to monetize. — [YouTube Help: Advertiser-friendly content guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)
- [3P-DATA] Kids & Teens median CPM $1.59, ads on only 32% of kids views, median RPM $0.33, versus Gaming at $2.05 RPM / $4.05 CPM (MilX/AIR, 300 channels, May 2025–May 2026). — [MilX](https://milx.app/en/guidelines/cpm-vs-rpm-the-key-metrics-for-youtube-monetization)
- [PRESS citing Kidscreen] Children's and family content is 15–20% of YouTube viewership but captures a disproportionately small share of ad spend. The kids ad market is about $4B, and "YouTube on its own cannot fill out the entire inventory." — [TechTimes, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320340/20260713/ai-kids-cartoon-gold-rush-has-hidden-tax-coppa-cuts-revenue-80.htm)
- [PRESS] CARU (the Children's Advertising Review Unit) scrutinized MrBeast's Feastables kids marketing, including a misleading "blind taste test" and possible child privacy violations (Sep 2025). — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/youtube-ads-influencer-mrbeast-raise-child-marketing-concerns)
- [PRESS] Kids panel (Precisify, Feb 2026): YouTube dominates US kids viewing. 80% of kids say they asked for something after seeing it advertised on YouTube. Gaming content preference rises from 39% (ages 6–9) to 43% (ages 10–12). — [Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/02/19/report-youtube-overwhelmingly-dominates-us-kids-viewing)
- [PRESS] Scholastic chose JJ & Mikey because of its family-friendliness: "We have such a hard time finding great properties that are appropriate for kids … especially on platforms such as YouTube," and "A lot of the gaming content on YouTube has no plot structure, but…" (story-driven was the differentiator). — [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/childrens/childrens-industry-news/article/100693-licensing-hotline-june-2026.html)
- [3P-DATA] Kids channels earn mainly from licensing, merch, brand partnerships and 24/7 live streams. One kids channel's live streams went from 58% to 85% of revenue in Q4 2025, with total revenue up 22% while views fell 23%. — [MilX kids guide](https://milx.app/en/guidelines/guide-to-monetize-videos-in-youtube-kids-niche)
- [3P-DATA] Minecraft creator sponsorship is uneven. Several top builders have a "little" sponsorship track record, while some challenge channels are "Strong" (sponsors such as Zenni, Gameloft, Pokemon). — [ThoughtLeaders](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/blog/best-minecraft-youtubers)

### Inferences
- For an *RPM-focused* Minecraft channel, the commercially durable position is general-audience or teen/adult content (building, tutorials, lore, survival series, update analysis), which earns gaming-level RPM and is protected by the Not Made for Kids status *only if it is genuinely not child-directed*. Kids-directed Minecraft animation can win huge view counts but earns roughly a sixth of the RPM. Its upside comes from owning a character IP that brands and licensors (pocket.watch, Scholastic, Marketplace) will pay for, which a fan-character channel can never do.
- Advertiser and regulator pressure (Fairplay, CARU, FTC, the NYT and WIRED investigations) targets exactly the low-effort, AI, kids-bait end of the market. That end has the shortest expected lifespan.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 advertiser survey specific to Minecraft or gaming brand safety.
- I found no YouTube-published CPM data by game or category. All RPM and CPM figures above are third-party panel medians and vary widely between sources (for example, Gyre says $1–3 for kids versus MilX's $0.33).
