## Q1. YouTube Partner Program: eligibility thresholds (incl. the 2027-02-01 change), the July 2026 "inauthentic content" clarification, AI voices/narration, altered/synthetic disclosure, reused content

### Takeaway
The 8,000-hour rule is real and official. From **1 Feb 2027**, new applicants need 1,000 subscribers plus **8,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days**, or 20M qualified Shorts views in 90 days. The fan-funding tier (500 subscribers + 3,000 hours) is unchanged. A channel starting in Nov 2026 has about 11 weeks to reach the old 4,000-hour bar, which is unrealistic for a new channel, so it should plan around 8,000 hours.

The July 2026 clarification adds no new rule. It names three non-monetizable buckets that bear directly on both niches:
- Generic/repetitive content, including "characters put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome". This is the main risk for AI-bot simulation series.
- Unsatisfying/off-putting content, including "repeatedly uses disturbing themes (such as violence or loss) without building a cohesive narrative". This is the main risk for horror.
- AI "expert" personas on sensitive topics.

AI tools, including AI voices, do not by themselves bar monetization. Disclosure is required only for realistic synthetic content, and disclosing does not reduce monetization.

### Cited Findings
**Thresholds (OBSERVED, official)**
- "Starting Feb. 1, 2027, YPP entry thresholds for new creators are changing to 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days, or 20M qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days, in addition to still needing 1k subscribers. If you are already in YPP, your status is not impacted by this update." — [YouTube Help 12843009](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
- The current rule (until 31 Jan 2027) is 1,000 subscribers with 4,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months, or 1,000 subscribers with 10M qualified Shorts views in 90 days. Watch hours from Shorts-feed views do not count toward the 4,000. — [YouTube Help 72851](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en)
- Fan funding (memberships, Super Chat), YouTube Creator Partnerships and Shopping stay at **500 subscribers + 3,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days, or 3M Shorts views in 90 days**. — [TeamYouTube announcement, 10 Aug 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/blog/451804019); [YouTube Blog, 10 Aug 2026](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youTube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn)
- **Shorts revenue from 1 Feb 2027:** a channel needs 10M qualified Shorts views over the last 90 days to share in Shorts ad/Premium revenue. Channels below that "remain in YPP and continue earning on long-form content". New incentive programs (Shopping bonuses, brand-deal incentives, trend boosts) are promised for channels under the bar, with details still to come. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youTube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn)
- **Activity rule from 1 Feb 2027:** a channel counts as active with 1,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days, **or** 1M Shorts views in 90 days, **or** 2 long-form videos or 5 Shorts every 90 days. Inactive channels get a 90-day window to recover. — [YouTube Help 12843009](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
- Creators must accept the new Watch Page / Shorts / Commerce modules by 31 Jan 2027 or stop earning from the related features. — [The Tech Outlook, 11 Aug 2026](https://www.thetechoutlook.com/news/apps/new-updates-announced-for-the-youtube-partner-program-few-additional-updates-released-for-ypp-terms/)
- New "Direct Shorts revenue": if an advertiser targets an ad to five or fewer channels, eligible creators get a direct 45% share. — [YouTube Help 12843009](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
- A third-party explainer flags an **open question**: whether a channel that crosses 4,000 hours before February but is still under review on 1 Feb is judged under the old or the new rule. — [Drawn Films, 4 Nov 2026](https://drawnfilms.com/blog/youtube-monetization-requirements/)

**Inauthentic content (OBSERVED)**
- Official page, dated 15 Jul 2025: "repetitious content" was renamed "inauthentic content" to clarify that it "includes content that is repetitive or mass-produced"; this content "has always been ineligible". The reused-content policy did not change. Monetized content must "Not be mass-produced, generic, repetitive, or manipulative."
  - Reviewers look at the channel's main theme, most-viewed videos, newest videos, biggest share of watch time, metadata (titles, thumbnails, descriptions) and the About section.
  - Source: [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en&p=inauthentic_content)
- **July 2026 clarification**, rolled out 16 Jul 2026 for all YPP members. "Any YouTube channel that has too much of any of these three types of content will not be able to monetize" (YouTube Trust & Safety VP Matt Halprin). — [TechCrunch, 20 Jul 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
- YouTube's own examples of the three categories (quoted by Tubefilter, which calls it "a communications fix", not new rules). Source: [Tubefilter, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update)
  1. **Generic or repetitive.** "content that looks like it's made with a template, or that may feel repetitive to viewers after watching several videos in a row from the same channel." Examples:
     - "videos where characters are put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome"
     - image slideshows and templated storylines
     - "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective"
     - The remedy YouTube names is the creator's transformative "spin".
  2. **Unsatisfying or off-putting.** "content that relies heavily on emotionally manipulative formulas, mimics existing formats or stories to a degree that the videos feel interchangeable, or appears designed to shock or surprise viewers for the sole purpose of getting views." Examples:
     - "Content that repeatedly uses disturbing themes (such as violence or loss) without building a cohesive narrative"
     - repetitive animals-in-distress series
     - "videos that stitch together unrelated or inconsistent AI clips to surprise or shock viewers"
     - realistic deceptive visuals (fake disaster or celebrity death)
  3. **AI personas on sensitive topics.** Content "that presents itself as a human expert providing advice to viewers on topics such as health, legal issues, finances, or politics."
- Halprin: AI "can allow you to make lots of videos really quickly that are very similar … don't really have a narrative arc … that's the stuff that we don't want to have in YPP." — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
- Halprin also said even tutorial videos can fall in the generic bucket if they are unoriginal content already common on the platform. — [Moneywise, 24 Jul 2026](https://moneywise.com/news/top-stories/youtube-ai-slop-inauthentic-content-monetization-rules)
- "A creator can use AI throughout the production process and remain eligible for monetization if the finished work contains an original story, useful information, entertainment, or another clear [value]." — [Mashable, 21 Jul 2026](https://mashable.com/life/youtube-ai-slop-monetization-policy)
- **Earlier statement (Jul 2025):** TeamYouTube said "channels that use AI in their content remain eligible to monetize". Its examples of mass production were "narrated stories with only superficial differences" and "slideshows that all have the same narration". — local note `sustainability_risk.md` citing [YouTube Community thread](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/356734251?hl=en&msgid=357092149)

**AI voice / synthetic disclosure (OBSERVED, official)**
- Disclosure is required only for realistic AI content: making "a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do", altering footage of a real event or place, generating "a realistic scene that didn't actually occur", or AI music that is the main focus. "Realistic AI content and meaningful changes require disclosure, while non-realistic or minor edits don't." — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)
- "Labels may appear in the expanded description for **non-music AI-generated audio** or AI content that is non-photorealistic or animated." "Disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money."
  - YouTube may auto-label content made with its own GenAI tools, content carrying C2PA metadata, or content "our internal systems detect is AI generated or altered". Auto-labels from YouTube tools, C2PA or manual review cannot be removed.
  - "Creators who consistently choose not to disclose … may be subject to … penalties … including removal of content or suspension from the YouTube Partner Program."
  - Source: [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)
- More prominent auto-labels for "significant photorealistic AI use" have applied since 27 May 2026. — local note citing [Thurrott](https://www.thurrott.com/a-i/336552/youtube-now-automatically-applies-more-visible-labels-on-ai-generated-videos)

**Reused content (OBSERVED)**
- Unchanged. Commentary, clips, compilations and reactions can monetize with significant original commentary, modification or value. — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)

### Inferences
- **Time to monetize (ESTIMATED).** 8,000 h = 480,000 watch minutes.
  - At an average view duration of 8–10 min (a 25–30 min video at roughly 30–35% retention), the channel needs about **48k–60k long-form views in 12 months**, with 1,000 subscribers.
  - Under the old 4,000 h rule it would need about 24k–30k.
  - A channel launched now will very likely apply after 1 Feb 2027, so the model should assume 8,000 h. Long videos with high retention are the cheapest way there; Shorts views do not count toward watch hours.
- **Horror (INFERRED).** The "off-putting" bucket targets disturbing themes *without a cohesive narrative*, interchangeable formula stories and shock-for-views. Serialized horror with an original monster, a real plot arc, character change and escalating, payoff-driven episodes sits on the safe side. Ten "monster jumpscare compilation" videos with the same beats would sit on the unsafe side.
- **AI-bot civilization (INFERRED).** The literal example "characters put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome" describes the default failure of agent-simulation channels: run N agents, same prompt, a timelapse each time. To be safe, each video needs:
  - a distinct experimental question,
  - visible creator analysis and commentary (the "spin"),
  - real narrative stakes,
  - outcomes that differ.
  Raw unedited agent logs or long silent timelapses are risky.
- **AI narration (INFERRED).** No official 2026 text bans TTS/AI voices. The risk is the combination of an AI voice, templated visuals and an interchangeable script. An original monster and world, an authored script and Blender/Minecraft visuals do not need the AI-use "Yes" flag, because none of it is realistic. A synthetic voice may still get a description-level label; that has no monetization effect according to YouTube.
- **Do not clone a real person's voice.** That triggers mandatory disclosure, plus impersonation and privacy rules.
- Reviewers weigh the *most-viewed* and *newest* videos and the thumbnails. One viral templated or overly graphic upload can colour the whole YPP review.

### Gaps
- No official statement found on whether a channel that crosses 4,000 h before 1 Feb 2027 but is reviewed after that date is grandfathered.
- No official text found that names TTS/AI narration specifically in the July 2026 clarification. The fetched excerpts of the help page did not include the full new example list, so the category examples above are via Tubefilter's quote of YouTube's text.
- YouTube publishes no counts of channels demonetized under inauthentic content.

## Q2. Advertiser-friendly guidelines for horror / gaming violence / shocking content, thumbnails and first seconds, profanity, and how mid-rolls work

### Takeaway
For gaming, the timing rule is what matters. As of the **September 2026** update:
- Graphic game violence **in the thumbnail or the first 7 seconds → no ads**.
- **In seconds 8–15 → limited ads.**
- **After 15 seconds** in normal gameplay → ads are fine.

"Unrealistic, playful" violence such as "running from monsters" is explicitly ad-friendly. Blocky Minecraft horror can therefore earn full ads if the opening and thumbnail avoid gore and the scares are tension rather than dismemberment.

Profanity at the start, or throughout most of the video, limits ads. A channel where most videos are unsuitable can lose ads channel-wide. Mid-rolls are available on videos of 8 minutes or longer; placement at natural breaks matters more than count.

### Cited Findings
- **September 2026 change (OBSERVED):** "We are updating our Violent Content guidelines to clarify content featuring graphic gaming content in the first eight to fifteen seconds will earn ad revenue. Content featuring graphic gaming content in the first seven seconds will continue to be ineligible for ad revenue." — [YouTube Help 9725604](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9725604?hl=en)
- **Current gaming rules (OBSERVED)**, from [Advertiser-friendly guidelines 6162278](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en&ref_topic=6151248&visit_id=639226316944285577-2976100721&rd=1) and [Gaming and monetization 10291745](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10291745?hl=en-419):
  - **Can earn ads:**
    - "Graphic scenes (such as a gory attack on a person) outside the first 15 seconds"
    - "Violence that is unrealistic, playful, and generally acceptable for all ages (such as family-friendly video games depicting running from monsters)"
    - obscured or blurred violence
    - "unedited gameplay violence occurring after the first 15 seconds"
  - **Limited ads:** "Graphic game violence in the thumbnail or in the first 8 to 15 seconds". "Graphic game violence" includes "brutal killings or severe injuries focusing on bodily fluids and parts such as beheadings and dismemberment."
  - **No ads:**
    - "Focus on gameplay manufactured to create a shocking experience", e.g. "Aggregating non-playable characters for mass killings"
    - "Graphic game violence in the thumbnail or in the first seven seconds"
    - gameplay showing sexual violence or hate-motivated violence
- **Note on a source conflict:** one cached version of page 6162278 still says "first 7 seconds" for the "can earn" tier, while the current versions say 15 s. The 9725604 changelog confirms the September 2026 wording. — [older cache](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en&sjid=7364696914463995013-EU) vs [current](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en&visit_id=639203298475509138-2104441959&rd=1)
- **Context rule (OBSERVED):**
  - "Violence in unedited video gameplay is generally acceptable for advertising, but montages where gratuitous violence is the focal point is not. All games (whether realistic or non-realistic) are in scope."
  - Policies apply to "video, Short, or live stream, thumbnail, title, description, and tags."
  - **Shocking:** "Content that may upset, disgust, or shock viewers may not be suitable … Unobscured shocking elements won't necessarily result in your video being unsuitable … but context matters."
  - **Profanity:** "Content that contains profanity or vulgarity at the start or throughout the majority of the video may not be suitable"; occasional use, including in background music, "won't necessarily" be.
  - **Channel-level penalty:** "We may reserve the right to disable ads on your entire channel in situations where the majority of your content is not suitable for any advertisers."
  - Source: [YouTube Help 6162278](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)
- Since 10 Mar 2025, ad-suitability checks can include a human review taking up to 24 h. — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- **Other 2026 ad-guideline updates (OBSERVED):**
  - Aug 2026: clarified depictions of death in educational/documentary content.
  - Sep 2026: gaming and scripted depictions of recreational drugs are now eligible for ads.
  - Oct 2026: certain public-interest content in the hateful/harmful categories is eligible.
  - Source: [YouTube Help 9725604](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9725604)
- **Sensitive events (OBSERVED):** civil emergencies, natural disasters, terrorism, conflict and mass violence; "applies even if the content contains no graphic imagery." — [YouTube Help 6162278](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)
- **Mid-rolls (OBSERVED):**
  - "On monetized videos that are 8 minutes or longer, you can turn on ads during the middle of the video."
  - Automatic placement, manual placement, or both are available.
  - "Mid-roll ad slots that can be disruptive … such as mid-sentence or mid-action are less likely to serve ads."
  - A mid-roll quality-feedback tool shows which slots are unlikely to serve.
  - Source: [YouTube Help 6175006](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6175006?hl=en&ref_topic=9257896)
  - Pre-roll and post-roll format choices were removed; all formats now run "when appropriate". — [YouTube Help 12929256](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12929256?hl=en&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dvideo)

### Inferences
- **Horror production rules for full ads (INFERRED from the official text):**
  - No blood, gore, mutilation or "body-horror" closeups in the **thumbnail or the first 15 s**.
  - Open on dread: atmosphere, a sound cue, the monster's silhouette.
  - Keep kills off-screen or cut away. Blocky characters "despawning" read as unrealistic and playful.
  - No profanity in the cold open; keep narration largely clean.
  - Avoid "jumpscare compilations". They fit both the montage-violence rule and the off-putting bucket.
- **A Minecraft-style original monster is an advantage.** "Unrealistic … running from monsters" is literally YouTube's green example. Realistic or photoreal gore (AI-rendered creepypasta) is the main way a Minecraft horror channel would fall into limited ads.
- **Civilization sims:**
  - Wars, massacres and genocide in-sim can trigger "manufactured shocking experience (mass killing NPCs)" if framed as spectacle.
  - Real-world-coded terrorism or current-conflict analogies can trigger sensitive events.
  - Frame conflict as systems and strategy, not carnage; avoid real-event parallels in titles and thumbnails.
- **Mid-rolls (INFERRED):**
  - For 20–40 min videos, write natural act breaks into the script (chapter cliffhangers in horror, "day N" transitions in civ sims) and place slots there. Automatic plus manual gives the system more choices.
  - YouTube states no maximum number of mid-rolls. How much revenue more breaks add is not published (see Q5 for the length effect).

### Gaps
- No official definition of "horror" as a category. Horror is judged under the violence, shocking and adult rules.
- No measured share of Minecraft horror videos receiving limited ads, and no creator report found this session of a Minecraft horror video getting the yellow icon.
- No official cap or recommended density for mid-rolls on very long videos (1 h+).

## Q3. Made for Kids: how YouTube decides for Minecraft content, RPM consequences, keeping horror/simulation clearly general-audience

### Takeaway
Minecraft is not MFK by default. YouTube's own FAQ lists "A Minecraft video that includes adult humor" as not necessarily made for kids. MFK turns on with child-directed signals: animated or cartoon characters, kid-level language, simple games, intended or actual child audience.

YouTube relies on the creator's setting but uses machine learning and may override it. MFK removes personalized ads, memberships, comments, notifications, end screens and merch. Measured median RPM is **$0.33 for Kids & Teens vs $2.05 for Gaming**.

Horror and civilization/AI themes, with mature framing, sit naturally on the "not made for kids" side. The risk is cute styling plus young-kid language.

### Cited Findings
- "if your video has actors, characters, activities, games, songs, stories, or other subject matter that reflects an intent to target children, it's likely 'made for kids'." A video is not necessarily MFK just because it is, for example, "An animated program that appeals to everyone" or "A Minecraft video that includes adult humor."
  - A 13+ disclaimer "doesn't mean that the FTC will automatically deem your content as not made for kids"; it is one factor among others.
  - Source: [YouTube Help 9684541 FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9684541?hl=en&ref_topic=9689353)
- **Factors (OBSERVED):**
  - subject matter
  - intended or actual audience
  - "characters, celebrities, or toys that appeal to children, including animated characters or cartoon figures"
  - "language … intended for children to understand"
  - kid activities
  - whether it is advertised to children
  - "Content that contains sexual themes, violence, obscene, or other mature themes not suitable for young audiences is likely not made for kids."
  - Under 13 = kid (US).
  - Source: [YouTube Help 9528076](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076?hl=en&ref_topic=10317451)
- "We'll also use machine learning to help us identify videos that are clearly directed to young audiences … We may need to override your audience setting choice in cases where we detect error or abuse." — [YouTube Help 9527654](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654?hl=en&ref_topic=10317451)
- **Features off on MFK:** personalized ads, cards/end screens, channel memberships, comments, donate button, merch and ticketing, notifications, Miniplayer, Save to playlist and Watch Later, Super Chat. On MFK channels: memberships, notifications, posts and comments are off. — [YouTube Help 9632097](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9632097?hl=en)
- Only contextual ads run on MFK content. Video-game ads rated unsuitable for under-13s are barred there. — [YouTube Help 9713557](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9713557?hl=en)
- **Measured RPM (OBSERVED):** AIR Media-Tech, 300 channels, 3,595 monetized channel-months, May 2025–May 2026.
  - Kids & Teens median **$0.33** (P25–P75 $0.16–$0.88).
  - Gaming median **$2.05**.
  - All-niche median $2.30.
  - Sources: [Mubert summary of AIR table, 27 Oct 2026](https://mubert.com/blog/youtube-rpm-by-niche-and-country); [AIR 2026 statistics](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-monetization-statistics-2026)
  - MilX/AIR data (local note): ads ran on only 32% of kids views, with a median kids CPM of $1.59. — [MilX](https://milx.app/en/guidelines/guide-to-monetize-videos-in-youtube-kids-niche) via `sustainability_risk.md`
- **Legal exposure (OBSERVED, local note):**
  - FTC/DOJ v. Disney: $10M penalty for mislabeling kid-directed YouTube videos as not-MFK (Sep/Dec 2025).
  - Per-violation COPPA penalty: $53,088.
  - Source: [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/court-approves-order-requiring-disney-pay-10-million-settle-ftc-allegations-firm-enabled-unlawful) via `sustainability_risk.md`

### Inferences
- **The RPM penalty for MFK is about 6× (INFERRED):** $2.05 → $0.33 using the two AIR niche medians. Losing memberships, notifications and comments also slows growth.
- **Keeping both niches clearly general-audience (13+/teen–adult), INFERRED from the factor list:**
  - Mature themes: dread, death, survival, ethics of AI, power and politics.
  - Adult-level narration vocabulary and pacing.
  - Darker grading.
  - No toy, nursery, color or "baby mob" framing in titles and thumbnails.
  - No "for kids" tags.
  - Set the channel to "No, not made for kids" deliberately and keep the metadata consistent.
  - Avoid mascot-cute monster designs.
  - Do not chase kid-skewed trends such as Maizen-style JJ & Mikey thumbnails.
- **Tension (INFERRED):** horror must stay mature enough to be clearly not-MFK, yet non-graphic enough for full ads (Q2). The sweet spot is psychological/atmospheric horror with no on-screen gore, comparable to a PG-13 film.
- AI civilization content has a natural teen/adult tech audience: AI-lab experiments and agent dynamics. It is the lower MFK risk of the two.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 creator report found of a Minecraft horror or simulation channel being reclassified MFK by YouTube's ML.
- YouTube's age-assurance rollout effects on teen ad personalization were not researched this session.

## Q4. Minecraft Usage Guidelines and EULA: monetizing videos, naming, mods and servers, selling mods, events with real players, bots on private servers

### Takeaway
Mojang allows ad-monetized Minecraft videos if they are free to view and contain "enough of your own unique content". Mojang can revoke that permission at any time. Other rules:
- Sponsor callouts must sit **outside game content** and be **suitable for children**.
- "Minecraft" may not be the primary or dominant word in the channel or series name.
- Content must not imply official status, and a "NOT AN OFFICIAL MINECRAFT …" disclaimer is required.

Mods: the EULA says original Java mods are yours "as long as you don't sell them for money / try to make money from them", so **selling mods directly is not allowed**. Servers can charge access under equal-price and all-ages rules.

Bots: no Mojang clause on AI agents or bots was found. Agent frameworks (Mindcraft/Mineflayer) need an official account per bot on online-mode servers, or run on LAN/offline local worlds.

### Cited Findings
**Videos (OBSERVED)** — source: [minecraft.net Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)
- "You may also make money from your videos and streams, through ad revenue". Conditions:
  - videos "free to view";
  - "You add enough of your own unique content … You can't just include your logo, web address, or overlays on top of our or someone else's gameplay – we reserve our right to decide on if your content is unique enough";
  - "You place any title card, end credits, sponsorship notices, and other callouts outside of the actual game content. Sponsors and any callouts must be suitable for children and minors and must not cause harm to the brand";
  - "You don't sell copies of the videos";
  - paid distribution (iTunes, Prime Video) is "not cool".
- Sharing creations counts as "commercial use" whether or not you get paid.
- "All permissions … may be revoked at any time if we think that it is appropriate to do so, or we don't like what you are doing."
- Required disclaimer: "NOT AN OFFICIAL MINECRAFT [PRODUCT/SERVICE/EVENT/etc.]. NOT APPROVED BY OR ASSOCIATED WITH MOJANG OR MICROSOFT".
- "Constructed promotions": brands, agencies and others may not use Minecraft gameplay to promote unrelated products without written approval. Disallowed example: "Minecraft blocks used to build a world or characters from a movie".
- "If something isn't covered by these guidelines … please don't do it without getting written permission from us."

**Naming (OBSERVED)**
- "You may not use the Minecraft name as the primary or dominant name or title." It may be used in a secondary description where needed to be honest. Mojang's own example: "Kotoba Miners: A Minecraft server for Redstone builds" is OK; "Minecraft - the ultimate Kotoba server for Redstone" is not.
- Sources: [mcrpg summary, read 3 Nov 2026](https://www.mcrpg.com/articles/minecraft-server-monetization-eula); local note `sustainability_risk.md` citing [minecraft.net Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines). This session's fetch excerpt did not include the naming paragraph.

**Mods (OBSERVED)**
- EULA: "Any Mods you create for Minecraft: Java Edition from scratch belong to you … and you can do whatever you want with them, **as long as you don't sell them for money / try to make money from them** and so long as you don't distribute Modded Versions of the game." Mods are fine to distribute; modded versions or hacked clients are not. — [Minecraft EULA](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/eula)
- Usage Guidelines: you may create and distribute a mod if you distribute "the mod only", with no play-to-earn mechanics and no checks for out-of-game ownership such as NFTs. — [Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)
- The EULA forbids making "commercial use of anything we've made" without permission; the Usage Guidelines are "extra permissions" that "often change". — [Minecraft EULA](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/eula)

**Servers and events (OBSERVED)** — source: [Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)
- You may charge for server access if the price is the same for everyone and payers get all enabled mods.
- Donations are allowed if donors get nothing exclusive; server-wide rewards are OK.
- Cosmetics (no capes) and non-pay-to-win entitlements are allowed.
- "All servers, entitlements, and advertising are suitable for audiences of all ages (for example, gambling, pornography, violence, terrorism, explicit lyrics, or other unsafe/mature content)".
- Server operators must make influencers disclose paid relationships per FTC rules.
- No hiding the EULA or other dialogs.
- Realms (Mojang-hosted) may not be monetized: you may not "receive financial, commercial or other benefits for letting other people play on your Realm". — [Minecraft EULA](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/eula)

**Bots (OBSERVED, third-party)**
- Mindcraft (LLM + Mineflayer): "To connect to online servers your bot will need an official Microsoft/Minecraft account … you will need another account if you want to connect too". The default setup is a local world opened to LAN.
  - It warns: "Do not connect this bot to public servers with coding enabled".
  - Source: [GitHub mindcraft-bots/mindcraft](https://github.com/mindcraft-bots/mindcraft)
- Mineflayer "works with both premium and offline (cracked) servers, depending on server settings". — [mineflayer.com](https://mineflayer.com/)
- One AI-agent project deliberately offers no offline mode because the EULA premise is that you bought the game. — [dsh-mc-agent README](https://github.com/hellosky983/dsh-mc-agent)

**Precedent: licensed fan mods from an original horror IP (OBSERVED)**
- ThatMob's scripted Minecraft horror ARG "Verity" (first episode 30 May 2026) launched an officially licensed Java adaptation on CurseForge (30 Jun 2026).
- Recreations passed 22.9M downloads (as of 8 Sep 2026, per a fan site).
- Source: [VerityMinecraft fan site](https://verityminecraft.com/) (unofficial; figures not independently verified)

### Inferences
- **Horror channel risk (INFERRED):** Mojang's video section does not ban mature themes in videos. The all-ages clause applies to servers and advertising, and brand harm is judged by Mojang. Avoid content Mojang could see as brand-harming (gore, sexual content) and keep sponsor reads child-suitable and outside gameplay footage. That second point also blocks mature sponsors (gambling, alcohol) on a Minecraft channel.
- **Naming (INFERRED):** use an original channel and series brand, e.g. "[Monster name] Files", with "a Minecraft horror story" only in the description or subtitle. Never put "Minecraft" first or dominant in the channel name or logo, and do not use Mojang logos or fonts in branding.
- **Monetizing the original monster beyond YouTube (INFERRED):**
  - Selling a mod is barred by the EULA.
  - Free mods with a donation, Patreon or "support me" link sit in a grey zone ("try to make money from them") that was not resolved this session.
  - Licensed **Marketplace** add-ons (Q5) are the sanctioned paid route.
  - Licensing the *original character* outside Minecraft (books, toys, other games) is unaffected because the IP is the owner's. Verity shows that an original monster from a Minecraft horror series can become a licensable property.
- **AI-bot civilization (INFERRED):**
  - Running agents in your own locally hosted world (LAN, or a private server you control) is the standard practice and avoids third-party server ToS.
  - Online-mode multi-bot runs need one licensed account per bot, which is a cost line.
  - Running an offline-mode server for bots is technically possible. Mojang's stance is unstated, and offline mode for *human* participants who don't own the game conflicts with the EULA's purchase premise.
  - Mixed events with real players: free private events are fine. If players are charged, the equal-price, all-ages and disclosure server rules apply. Each player needs their own licensed account.
- **AI scripts and voices (INFERRED):** the Usage Guidelines give Mojang discretion over whether content is "unique enough". A channel of raw agent timelapses with an overlay is the riskiest case. Authored narrative, analysis and original builds are the safest.

### Gaps
- No Mojang statement found on AI agents or LLM bots in Minecraft, or on AI-generated Minecraft content specifically.
- Not resolved: whether a free mod with a Patreon or tip jar counts as "try[ing] to make money" under the EULA.
- The naming clause was not in this session's fetch excerpt. It is quoted from a secondary site and a prior local fetch.

## Q5. Revenue benchmarks 2025–2026 and other income streams

### Takeaway
The only measured 2026 panel puts **Gaming median RPM at $2.05** (P25–P75 $0.70–$3.62; 300 channels, May 2025–May 2026). The all-niche median is $2.30. Across that panel the median peaked at $2.35 in November and fell to $1.56 in January.

Minecraft-specific RPMs are estimates or anecdotes only: $3–$5 (vidIQ), $4–$8 for 20–30 min videos (one creator). Geography and length are the main levers (US/Tier-1 roughly 2× global; long videos allow multiple mid-rolls).

Non-ad income can match or exceed AdSense:
- hosting/game sponsors (gaming sponsor CPM benchmarks $10–$25, estimated)
- Apex Hosting affiliate (15% recurring)
- Minecraft Affiliate Program (5%)
- memberships (from 500 subscribers)
- Marketplace, which is gated: registered business, 18+, portfolio, usually via a publisher
- licensing an original IP

## Q6. 2025–2026 cases of Minecraft channels demonetized for AI/mass-produced content or horror ad limits

### Takeaway
No named, verifiable Minecraft channel was found demonetized under the inauthentic-content policy, or limited for horror, in 2025–2026. This repeats the 2026-11-04 local finding. Enforcement evidence exists for AI-slop and kids channels generally, including deletions and YPP suspensions. YouTube's CEO cited Minecraft culture as creativity to protect.

### Cited Findings
- Kapwing (Nov 2025): 278 all-AI-slop channels with 63B views and about $117M/yr in revenue. YouTube deleted 16 of the top 100 AI channels between Nov 2025 and Jan 2026. — local note citing [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2025/dec/27/more-than-20-of-videos-shown-to-new-youtube-users-are-ai-slop-study-finds) and [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/news/869684/youtube-top-ai-channels-removed-kapwing)
- Feb 2026: YouTube suspended 5 AI kids channels from YPP after a NYT investigation. Dexerto reported false positives, including an AI-slop detector hitting Kurzgesagt. — local note citing [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-strikes-down-channels-after-investigation-into-bizarre-ai-content-for-kids-3326231)
- A creator told Digiday his channel was *terminated* in the July 2025 repetitive-content crackdown. — local note citing [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-has-creators-concerned-marketers-cheering)
- Neal Mohan (14 May 2026) cited Minecraft culture as "emerging forms of creativity" YouTube doesn't want to suppress, while promising little prevalence of AI slop. — local note citing the [MoffettNathanson transcript](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_events/2026/May/14/Neal-Mohan-MoffettNathanson-Transcript-05-14-26.pdf)
- This session's searches for Minecraft horror limited ads and for Minecraft channels demonetized for reused or inauthentic content returned only policy pages and general explainers, with no named Minecraft cases. — [search context: YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [TubeChef, 8 Nov 2026](https://tubechef.ai/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy)

### Inferences
- The lack of named cases does not mean low risk. Enforcement is mostly silent: YPP rejections and limited ads are private to the creator.
- The closest analogues are mass-produced AI story and kids channels, so the AI-bot simulation niche, if templated, is closer to known enforcement patterns than authored horror.
- **Rejection math (ESTIMATED):** a YPP rejection usually means re-applying after 30 days, or 90 days after repeated rejections. That is older, commonly cited guidance and was not re-verified this session. Under the 8,000-hour regime, a rejection delays income by months, so the first ~30 uploads should be built to pass review: varied, authored, with clean thumbnails and openings.

### Gaps
- No Minecraft-specific demonetization case was found from any source.
- The YPP re-application waiting periods (30/90 days) were not re-verified on the official page this session.
- Reddit was not searched directly for horror-creator yellow-icon reports because of the tool budget.
